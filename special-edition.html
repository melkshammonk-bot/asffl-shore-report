ASFFL Owner’s Manual

How to Run a Fantasy Team Without Making It Your Full-Time Job

Five minutes, a few times a week, and absolutely no accounting degree required.

Prepared by The Shore Report Department of Helpful Interference

AMHERST SHORE — Fantasy football can sound complicated because experienced managers use twelve abbreviations to describe a decision that amounts to “put the healthy person in.” It is not complicated. You do not need to know every NFL player, watch every game or maintain Grant’s presumed six-tab FAAB spreadsheet.

You can manage a perfectly respectable team in about five minutes, two or three times a week. The object is simply to put active players into your starting lineup, improve your roster when a useful player becomes available and enjoy having a completely unnecessary reason to care about the fourth quarter of a Jacksonville game.

The Whole Fantasy Thing in 30 Seconds

1Real NFL players earn points from what they do in real games.
2Your starters’ points are added together each week.
3Score more than your opponent and behave as though it was entirely skill.

Your bench players still belong to you, but their points do not count. Each week, you decide who starts and who sits. Sleeper gives every player a projected score to help. The projection will sometimes be wrong, but it is considerably better than selecting players by uniform colour or Giants loyalty.

The Weekly Routine

This is the entire maintenance schedule. Anything beyond it is enthusiasm, anxiety or Kyle building another tool.

Tuesday Night

Look for injuries and submit waiver claims before bed. Ask for any free player who would improve your team. Your claims process at 4:00 a.m. Wednesday Atlantic time, while sensible people are asleep.

Wednesday

See what you won. Sleeper automatically awards players to the highest winning bids. After waivers clear, useful unclaimed players may be added as free agents.

Thursday

Check for Thursday players. If one is starting, confirm that he is active before kickoff. Once his game begins, that player is locked in place.

Sunday Morning

Perform the five-minute inspection. Replace anyone marked OUT or on a bye, compare close decisions using projections, and make sure every starting slot is filled.

Monday

Enjoy or endure. Watch the final game, calculate impossible comeback scenarios and remember that none of this affects the family Christmas invitation list.

The Three Rules That Prevent Most Disasters

1. Never leave an empty starting slot.

2. Replace players marked OUT or BYE.

3. Make changes before that player’s NFL game begins. There are no automatic substitutes in this league.

What Exactly Is a FLEX?

A FLEX is an extra starting spot that can hold a running back, wide receiver or tight end. It cannot hold a quarterback, kicker or defense. We have two FLEX spots.

A useful habit: put the player whose game starts latest in FLEX. If late injury news arrives, FLEX gives you more replacement options. This is not mandatory, but it is one of those tiny moves that makes you look suspiciously competent.

Waivers: Shopping While Everyone Is Sleeping

Players who are not on a team are usually either on waivers or are free agents. A free agent can be added immediately. A player on waivers must be requested, and everyone gets a fair chance to bid.

  1. Find the player in Sleeper and tap Add.
  2. If your roster is full, choose a player to drop.
  3. Enter a FAAB bid—even $0 is allowed.
  4. Submit it by Tuesday night.
  5. Check Wednesday morning to see whether you won.

A dropped player stays on waivers for two days, preventing somebody from dropping and instantly re-adding players to create recreational chaos.

FAAB: Your $100 for the Entire Season

FAAB means Free Agent Acquisition Budget. Every team begins with $100 for the whole season. It is imaginary money, there is no weekly spending limit, and unused FAAB earns no interest and cannot be applied to the pizza bill.

You bid secretly. If Grant bids $11 and you bid $12, you get the player and pay $12. Grant records the outcome in the appropriate ledger and moves on professionally.

$0–$5A temporary fill-in, kicker, defense or modest upgrade.
$6–$15A player who could become a regular starter.
$16–$30+A major breakout or rare player who could change your season.

These are guidelines, not laws. Spend more when the player would genuinely help you for several weeks. Spend less when you merely recognize his name from television.

Injuries, Byes and IR

Q means questionable: check again closer to kickoff. D means doubtful: prepare a replacement. O means out: move the player out of your starting lineup. Lakota may reasonably object that “questionable” provides no useful legal certainty, but the NFL has declined to revise its terminology.

A bye week is a scheduled week off for an NFL team. A player on bye is healthy but scores zero, which is impressively similar to being unavailable.

The ASFFL has four IR spots. If Sleeper marks a player as eligible, moving him to IR can free a regular roster spot while you wait for him to return. Sleeper will tell you who qualifies; no medical degree is required.

The Sunday Morning Decision Machine

Is every starting position filled?
If no: fill it.
Is anyone OUT or on a BYE?
If yes: replace them.
Is anyone QUESTIONABLE?
If yes: check the latest status.
Still choosing between two healthy players?
Start the higher projection and enjoy breakfast.

Trades Without a Law Degree

A trade exchanges players between two teams. You may accept, reject or counter. You never have to accept a trade, and “no thanks” is a complete response—even if Paul has attached a four-paragraph explanation of why the offer helps you.

A good beginner’s test is simple: does the trade improve your weekly starting lineup, and can you afford what you are giving away? Do not judge it by the number of players alone. Two average bench players are not automatically worth one excellent starter.

The trade deadline is the league’s final date for completing trades. After it passes, trading closes, but lineup changes, waivers and free-agent additions continue. Check Sleeper or ask Kyle, Nick or Oliver for the current deadline.

The Glossary You Can Pretend You Already Knew

Starter
A player whose points count this week.
Bench
Your reserves. Their points do not count unless you start them.
Projection
An estimate of a player’s points. Useful guidance, not a guarantee.
Matchup
Your team against one other ASFFL team for the week.
Waivers
The overnight system for fairly awarding available players.
FAAB
Your season-long $100 budget for waiver bids.
Free Agent
An unrostered player who can be added immediately.
Add/Drop
Add one player and release another to make room.
Bye
An NFL team’s scheduled week off. Its players score zero.
Lock
Once a player’s game starts, he cannot be moved that week.
PPR
Points per reception: a scoring format that rewards catches.
DEF / DST
An NFL team’s defense and special teams, used as one starter.
K
Kicker. Often ignored until one scores 18 points against you.
IR
Reserve space for players Sleeper designates as eligible.
