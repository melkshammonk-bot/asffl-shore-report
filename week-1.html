A dead team scores 197.85

Paul spent the draft in Albania. He missed the oceanfront, the Post-it-note operation, and Oliver’s pizza. Apparently none of these are required to win a fantasy football game.

The defending champion’s The Trading Block beat Grant’s Boot Tuten Boogie 197.85–196.85 in a rematch of last year’s final. That is 394.70 points between them, with the entire championship sequel decided by one.

Throughout Sunday, Paul kept messaging Grant to announce that his team was dead. It was an unusual diagnosis for a team that would finish with the highest score in the league. Kenneth Walker contributed 33.10, and by the time the patient was discharged, Grant had scored more than eight other managers and acquired an 0–1 record.

Grant may want to review the paperwork. Then again, Grant reviews the paperwork when he wins.

There was one consolation: he outscored Carlie. Unfortunately for Grant, Carlie was interested in outscoring him too—and unlike Grant, she won the game she was actually playing. Drake & Bake beat Sarah’s Dartastic 154.00–123.50, led by 28.30 from Bijan Robinson. The two household franchises can discuss the distinction between “more points” and “a win” while their dogs remain admirably indifferent.

Sarah got 27.90 from Jaxson Dart. The Giants Employment Program is off to a promising individual start, even if the team is 0–1. Sarah’s authority within the family remains wholly outside the jurisdiction of the standings.

Monday night, in two-tenths

Kyle needed 2.2 points from Jaylen Waddle on Monday night. Waddle gave him 0.2.

For those who do not regularly follow fantasy football, this is rather like asking someone to bring home a loaf of bread and receiving two crumbs. Kyle named his team Quack Quack and drafted Waddle without noticing the theme. Monday was a poor time for the team name to become funnier than the lineup.

Lakota stayed up to see every last minute of Giant Expectations’ 139.15–137.25 win over Quack Quack. Kyle stayed up too, presumably in case Waddle decided to do absolutely anything. He did not do quite enough.

There is an extra twist. Lakota had dropped Minnesota’s defense without realizing new additions were locked. Kyle used his co-commissioner access to put Las Vegas on her roster—a cagey bit of roster management that the transaction record calls a commissioner move, not Lakota’s waiver claim. The Raiders scored 18.00 in her lineup.

Kyle sorted out the defense and then watched it help beat him by 1.90 points. Fantasy football appreciates a tidy solution, even when its author does not.

Nick spent the offseason doing mock drafts with Lakota. She now has a custom team shirt, a Week 1 win, and reason to believe the training worked. Nick may soon discover that coaching the opposition has consequences.

No notes, no mercy

Ken arrived at the draft without notes and has opened the season by beating his son Oliver 138.00–94.70. Derrick Henry scored 35.30 for Big Apple; the 43.30-point margin was the largest of the week.

Oliver, who fed everyone homemade pizza at the draft, received no family discount. His starters George Kittle, Ja’Marr Chase, and Colston Loveland combined for 5.40 points. These are three names you would be delighted to find on a roster and somewhat less delighted to find on a Week 1 scoreboard.

Assistant GM Haleigh may request a meeting. Ken will presumably attend without preparing for it and leave with a win.

Luc also begins 0–1. Nick’s Bam Bam Cam beat Smack Dak ASB 174.40–137.05, with Josh Allen supplying 38.00. Luc got 25.70 from Amon-Ra St. Brown, which is a good contribution and, as it turns out, not a complete defense against Josh Allen. His Cowboys allegiance remains under its usual separate investigation.

Five managers have wins: Paul, Lakota, Carlie, Ken, and Nick. Grant has the league’s most painful loss, Kyle has two-tenths of a receiver, and Oliver still makes the best pizza at Amherst Shore.

Week 1 has provided enough material for a newspaper. Whether it has provided anyone a good night’s sleep is another matter.
