THE SHORE REPORT

Week 11 Tuesday Reaction | November 24, 2026

Do Your Job

Bill Belichick visits The Shore Report, three managers fall victim to Monday Night Football, and management discovers it may have hired the wrong person as its senior football analyst.

With the ASFFL playoff race entering its final three weeks, management decided it was time to bring in someone with championship experience. Unfortunately, our budget eliminated most of the available candidates, but following some negotiations that management has declined to discuss, six-time Super Bowl-winning head coach Bill Belichick arrived at The Shore Report offices Tuesday morning.

Bill entered through the opening where our front door used to be, surveyed the office and asked whether we were in the middle of renovations.

"No," explained Gordie. "Cam Skattebo."

Bill nodded, pulled up a chair and asked for the standings.

Gordie had barely slept Monday night after learning that Bill Belichick would be visiting The Shore Report. He'd spent most of his lunch break in the construction trailer telling the crew about it and had even turned down an opportunity for overtime on Tuesday. As he explained to management, opportunities to talk football with Bill Belichick don't come along very often on the side of Highway 6.

Armed with a handwritten list of questions and wearing his newly modified sleeveless hoodie, Gordie pulled up a chair beside his football hero.

Gordie: Bill, I've always wanted to ask you, what's it like to win a Super Bowl?

Belichick: Better than losing one.

Gordie: Of all six championships with New England, which one meant the most to you personally?

Belichick: The next one.

Gordie: But there wasn't a next one after the sixth.

Belichick: Correct.

Undeterred, Gordie moved further down his handwritten list.

Gordie: What's Tom Brady really like behind closed doors?

Belichick: Depends on the door.

Gordie: Did you ever get nervous before a big game?

Belichick: We prepared.

Gordie: That's exactly how I approach my weekly fantasy predictions. Preparation is everything.

Belichick: What's your record?

Gordie: Thirty-one and fourteen.

Belichick: What happened fourteen times?

Gordie had not anticipated being asked questions himself and was briefly thrown off his game.

Gordie: I've actually got a couple of Patriots fans in our league, Grant and Carlie. They're going to be absolutely thrilled when they find out you're here. Would you mind signing something for them?

Belichick: What?

Gordie: I was thinking my STOP/SLOW sign. I've got it out in the truck.

Belichick: No.

Fortunately, Lakota arrived with the week's research before Gordie could retrieve the sign. She placed the matchup summaries in front of Bill and mentioned that three ASFFL games had been decided on Monday night, with special teams playing a significant role in two of them.

"Really?" Bill replied, reaching for the paperwork. "Walk me through that."

Gordie couldn't believe it. He'd been sitting with his football hero for ten minutes, and Lakota had already gotten more out of him in fifteen seconds.

Three Games, One Monday Night

There were three ASFFL matchups still undecided when Cincinnati and Washington took the field Monday evening. By the time Washington kicked the winning field goal, Sarah, Carlie and Lakota had all secured victories, although each had taken a considerably different route to get there.

Bill was particularly interested in the contribution of special teams, a subject that has traditionally received approximately as much attention at The Shore Report as workplace safety regulations.

As it turns out, we probably should have been paying closer attention.

Dartastic 135.15 — I Love Tuten 126.35

Sarah's Giants Employment Program Is Hiring Again

After beginning the season 0–7, Sarah has now won three of her last four games. Chuba Hubbard contributed 26 points, Jared Goff added 23.15, and kicker Drew Stevens delivered an outstanding 18-point Monday night performance to put Dartastic over the top.

Following last week's newspaper coverage of Cam Skattebo's 26.20 points on Sarah's bench, Cam was pleased to discover he had been promoted to the starting lineup.

He responded with 2.80 points.

Management has elected not to notify Cam of this week's article until after a replacement front door has been installed.

For Luc, it was another frustrating afternoon. Dak Prescott produced 39.15 points, bringing his two-week total to an extraordinary 77, yet I Love Tuten still couldn't find enough production elsewhere.

Gordie suggested that Luc might need to reconsider his lineup strategy.

"Actually, he couldn't have done anything differently," Lakota explained. "His starting lineup was the highest-scoring legal combination available to him. There wasn't a single bench adjustment that would have won him the game."

Bill looked up from the statistics.

"Good observation. You evaluate the decision separately from the result."

"Exactly. Luc made the right selections. He just didn't have enough points available to select."

Luc may take some comfort in knowing that a six-time Super Bowl champion has effectively cleared him of managerial negligence.

Unfortunately, neither Bill nor Lakota could find him a fourth win.

Drake & Bake 154.40 — Sleezy Sandbars 145.50

Carlie Discovers Life Beyond Drake

With Drake Maye unavailable and the Patriots on their bye, Carlie turned to C.J. Stroud, who delivered 22.40 points. Rookie running back Jonah Coleman added 32.90, and the Denver defense contributed another 18.

But the real entertainment arrived Monday night.

With less than a minute remaining, Oliver was ahead 145.50–141.00. Cincinnati's Evan McPherson then kicked a 56-yard field goal, giving Carlie five points and a half-point lead. Washington receiver Antonio Williams added another 8.40 on the ensuing possession, securing the highest team score of the week.

Carlie managed to benefit from both sides of the same NFL game in its closing moments.

"That's why fantasy football is so interesting," Lakota observed. "Carlie needed Cincinnati to score, then immediately needed Washington to move the ball. She wasn't rooting for a team. She was rooting for a sequence of events."

Bill considered that for a moment.

"Situational football."

"Exactly."

Gordie attempted to contribute by explaining that he'd once successfully predicted the sequence of vehicles through a particularly complicated four-way intersection.

Neither analyst requested further details.

For Oliver, the circumstances were brutal. His 145.50 points would have beaten every other team in the ASFFL this week. Even his best available lineup wouldn't have beaten Carlie.

He has now lost three consecutive games despite producing some genuinely competitive scores.

Haleigh is encouraged to retain the receipts from this one. Oliver actually has a legitimate defense.

Bam Bam Cam 132.10 — The Trading Block 118.10

The Champion Is Experiencing Technical Difficulties

Paul remains the league's highest-scoring manager, but the defending champion has now lost two consecutive games, producing the lowest score in the league this week.

Nick, meanwhile, received 25.40 points from Travis Kelce, 24.10 from Javonte Williams and 22.10 from DJ Moore, overcoming an afternoon in which DK Metcalf was ejected and several other starters contributed very little.

Nick deserves credit for some important lineup adjustments. Compared with his Friday selections, his subsequent changes added a net 15.70 points. Without those changes, Paul would have won.

Bill approved.

"Preparation. Make the necessary adjustments. That's your job."

Lakota agreed, although she had brought along some additional statistics.

"Nick is 7–4, but if he'd played every other manager every week, his record would be 49–50. He's outscored his opponents by only 5.05 points across the entire season."

Bill leaned forward.

"So the record is better than the underlying performance."

"Significantly."

Gordie reminded Lakota that she was discussing her fiancé.

"I'm aware. We're playing each other next week."

There was a brief silence in the newsroom.

"Good preparation," Bill concluded.

Management would like Nick to know that none of the research files were obtained through unauthorized access to household devices.

We would also like to remind him that the DJ Moore–Keon Coleman trade investigation remains officially closed, although Moore's 22.10 points against Coleman's 3.70 have resulted in Lakota adding a supplementary document to the file.

She insists it is for completeness.

Big Apple 141.55 — Quack Quack 125.25

The Apple Doesn't Fall Far From the Playoffs

Ken has now won three consecutive games and accumulated 498.35 points over that period, moving Big Apple into fifth place and within striking distance of the postseason.

Last week, Christian McCaffrey contributed 46.30 points. This week, he managed just 13.20, yet Ken still won comfortably, with seven starters reaching double figures.

Lakota was particularly impressed by Ken's decision to insert Carnell Tate into his lineup and noted that, for the second consecutive week, Ken had selected his highest-scoring available legal lineup.

"That's good roster management," Bill said.

Management immediately launched an internal investigation into whether Ken had been conducting research.

Ken has previously denied allegations of this nature.

The Shore Report respects his position, although the accumulating evidence is becoming increasingly difficult to ignore.

For Kyle, Jahmyr Gibbs, Breece Hall and Parker Washington combined for 62.10 points, but there simply wasn't enough production elsewhere. Even a combination of Jayden Daniels and Jalen Coker replacing their lower-scoring counterparts would have left Quack Quack 2.40 points short.

Kyle currently ranks third in total points scored, yet finds himself seventh in the standings.

"That's another example of the schedule influencing the standings," Lakota explained.

Kyle has requested that management feature this observation prominently.

Management has declined his subsequent request to have the playoff qualification rules reviewed retrospectively.

Giant Expectations 131.75 — Boot Tuten Boogie 130.45

Grant's Private Consultation With Bill Belichick

We saved this week's Game of the Week until last, partly because it was the closest matchup of the weekend and partly because management was curious to see how Grant would respond to receiving professional football advice from one of his childhood heroes.

Grant entered Monday night with a 17.20-point lead, with Stefon Diggs remaining against Lakota's Tee Higgins and Mike Gesicki.

With two minutes remaining in the fourth quarter, Lakota still trailed 130.45–127.55.

Then came Gesicki's 32-yard reception.

The reception earned 3.20 points for the yardage and an additional point under the ASFFL's long-reception scoring rules. Those 4.20 points put Lakota ahead by 1.30, and Grant never recovered.

For anyone wondering whether one of those obscure scoring rules could ever determine a meaningful fantasy matchup, we now have our answer.

Bill asked Lakota whether she considered the victory a product of good management or good fortune.

"A bit of both," she replied. "Grant actually had several ways to beat me. Ladd McConkey was in his lineup Friday, but he eventually went with Omarion Hampton. McConkey scored 18.70, Hampton scored 9.30. If he'd stuck with McConkey, Grant would have beaten me by 8.10."

"Why'd he change it?"

"McConkey was questionable with a back injury. I understand the concern, but both players were in the same game. Once McConkey was confirmed active, Grant still had the option."

Bill studied the lineup.

"What about your team?"

"My quarterback and two other starters combined for 16.15 points. I wouldn't exactly be distributing championship rings based on that performance."

For the first time during his visit, Bill appeared to smile.

"Most people don't spend that much time finding problems with their own wins."

"Well, I'm trying to win the league, not just this week's argument."

Bill nodded.

"Good answer."

Gordie, sensing an opportunity to contribute, reminded everyone that he had correctly predicted Lakota to win.

"You said by eight," Lakota replied.

"Well, the important thing is that I identified the winner."

"Is that how we're measuring your performance now?"

Gordie requested that the remaining discussion take place in the presence of his union representative.

The Playoff Picture: Nobody Is Safe

With three weeks remaining, the standings are becoming increasingly uncomfortable.

2 Paul 7–4
3 Nick 7–4
4 Grant 6–5
5 Ken 6–5
6 Oliver 6–5
7 Kyle 5–6
8 Carlie 3–8
9 Luc 3–8
10 Sarah 3–8

Perhaps the most extraordinary statistic of the season is that Grant currently occupies the final playoff position ahead of Ken by 0.25 points scored across eleven weeks of football.

They play each other this week.

But Lakota had uncovered something even more interesting.

Despite leading the league at 9–2, she hasn't mathematically clinched a playoff berth. Conversely, Carlie, Luc and Sarah have already been eliminated, even though they could theoretically finish with six wins.

When Bill asked her to explain, Lakota pointed to the remaining schedule.

"Grant and Ken have to play each other this week. One of them gets to seven wins, or they both finish with a tie. Either way, four teams will be beyond the maximum the bottom three can achieve."

"And you?"

"If I lose all three remaining games, there's still a combination of results where five teams finish with at least nine wins. I'd have to rely on points scored."

"So what's the solution?"

"Beat Nick."

"Correct."

Gordie immediately began writing down Bill's advice.

Lakota also pointed out that Carlie, despite being eliminated, plays Kyle, Grant and Nick over the final three weeks. Having just recorded the highest score in the league, she could have an enormous influence on who makes the playoffs.

Carlie may no longer be able to win the championship, but she is perfectly capable of deciding who doesn't.

Grant is advised to keep this in mind when requesting household assistance over the coming weeks.

Gordie's Performance Review

Our senior predictions columnist finished Week 11 with a respectable 3–2 outright record, although only two of his five selections covered his published margins.

His reconstructed season record across nine published editions stands at 31–14 outright and 28–17 against the lines.

Management considers those respectable numbers.

Gordie considers them grounds for immediate removal from his Performance Improvement Plan.

Lakota considers them one component of a broader performance assessment.

Bill was asked for his professional opinion.

"What's the rest of the assessment?"

"Reader engagement and attitude," Lakota explained.

Bill looked at Gordie.

"Keep the plan."

One Final Question

As our guest prepared to leave, management asked whether he would consider returning to The Shore Report at some point in the future.

Bill didn't immediately answer. Instead, he turned to Lakota.

"You put all this together?"

"Most of the analysis, yes."

"You watch a lot of football?"

"Enough. Although around here, I spend a surprising amount of time explaining it to people who get paid to write about it."

Bill looked across the room at Gordie, who was still wearing his sleeveless hoodie.

"Yeah. I noticed."

Before leaving, Bill asked Lakota who she considered the biggest threat to her championship ambitions.

She glanced at the standings.

"Paul has scored the most points. Ken's the hottest team in the league. Oliver is playing better than his recent results suggest. And Carlie just reminded everyone what happens when you underestimate a 3–8 team."

"And Nick?"

"I'll have a better answer for you next Tuesday."

Bill smiled.

"On to Week Twelve."

Following his departure, management received a formal request from Gordie to clarify whether Lakota's responsibilities now included football analysis.

Management is reviewing the request.

In the meantime, readers are invited to settle an argument that developed in the newsroom this afternoon:

With three weeks remaining, who is the most dangerous team in the ASFFL that nobody wants to face in the playoffs?

Lakota has her opinions. Gordie has his.

For once, we'd quite like to hear yours.
