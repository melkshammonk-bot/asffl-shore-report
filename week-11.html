The Pylon's Lines — Week 11: Help Wanted. Help Arrived.

By Gordie "The Pylon" MacNeil · November 20, 2026

Got a call from the editor Wednesday informing me that my suspension had been lifted, although technically I was still on a Performance Improvement Plan until the end of November. Apparently that Poilievre fellow had reviewed my case and recommended reinstatement. Also recommended firing the editor, but management decided to implement his recommendations in stages.

I asked whether I'd be getting paid for the time I was suspended. Editor said that was under review. Told him I'd like to review his cheque sometime.

Then he informed me they'd hired somebody to improve my punctuality, reader engagement, and general attitude. Thursday came and went without a column, which I believe demonstrates how well the new system is working.

Anyway, went down to the newspaper Friday and found a young woman sitting at my desk, examining my Performance Improvement Plan. Name's Lakota. Apparently she'd been walking past the office Tuesday when she saw Pierre Poilievre leaving the building and decided to stop in to see what he was doing there.

With Cam Skattebo having removed the front door on Saturday, there wasn't much stopping her.

She mentioned she was preparing for law school, and the editor immediately handed her my personnel file. Within ten minutes, she'd been appointed some kind of reader-engagement consultant, despite having come in merely to ask a question.

I asked if she knew anything about football. Turns out she's leading this fantasy league at 8–2. I asked if she knew anything about newspapers. She said she'd read the last three editions and thought the writing was pretty good.

I thanked her.

She explained she particularly enjoyed the ones I hadn't written.

Been a difficult month.

Anyway, Lakota's reviewed my employment documents and says there are several irregularities. Apparently my performance objectives aren't measurable, the disciplinary procedures weren't followed, and the editor hasn't actually signed half the paperwork.

I told her that sounded promising.

She asked whether I had copies of my employment contract.

I said I had a clipboard.

She looked at it for a moment and asked whether it came with a pension.

I told her it had survived three foremen, two budget cuts, and an ice storm in Antigonish.

She said it was probably the most secure employee in the building.

First person I've met at this newspaper in years who appears to have a plan.

Now, five matchups. Four wins and one loss the last time I was allowed to make predictions. Meanwhile, that Skattebo fellow went two and three and cost the newspaper a door.

Lakota asked if I'd considered bringing him back.

I said absolutely not.

She said she only meant to finish the renovations.

I'm not saying I like her yet. But she's growing on me.

I Love Tuten (3–7) vs. Dartastic (2–8)

Luc's changed his team name again. Used to be I Love Pitts, which was based on a running back named Love and a tight end named Pitts. Now it's I Love Tuten.

Apparently he's decided that if a relationship isn't working, the solution is to change the name on the mailbox.

Fair enough. I've been considering something similar with the road crew.

Luc scored nearly 146 points last week behind Dak Prescott and Bhayshul Tuten, while Sarah has won two of her last three and finally decided to put Cam Skattebo in her starting lineup.

Good timing. Cam spent most of Saturday complaining about being benched, then apparently discovered that writing newspaper columns is considerably more hazardous than playing football.

Sarah's got Hubbard, Barkley, and enough running backs to keep a small farm operating. Luc has Prescott and St. Brown, and I like his chances if the offense continues producing.

Lakota says I should ask readers a question to improve engagement.

All right, here's one: How many times can Luc rename his team before somebody asks for identification?

She said that was a good start, although a question usually works better if you actually want to hear the answer.

I told her I'd worked for the government for forty years and had never seen any evidence of that.

The Pylon's Line: Luc by 6.

Sleezy Sandbars (6–4) vs. Drake & Bake (2–8)

Oliver's lost two straight and finds himself outside the playoff positions, which is unfortunate considering he was sitting comfortably in third not long ago.

Carlie's lost six straight, although she's been scoring enough points to make the whole situation particularly irritating. She finally started C.J. Stroud on Thursday and got 22.40 points, along with another 14.80 from Nico Collins.

Thirty-seven points before most of the league had finished supper. Nothing wrong with that.

Oliver's counting on Burrow and Chase again, and they've been reliable enough that I'd probably trust them to finish paving the highway if the relief crew didn't show up.

Of course, Oliver's also got three tight ends in his starting lineup. Three. I've worked on road crews with fewer people assigned to hold signs.

Lakota tells me that lineup is perfectly legal.

I told her legality and common sense aren't necessarily the same thing.

She said she'd already reached the same conclusion after reading my Performance Improvement Plan.

Starting to like this arrangement.

The Pylon's Line: Oliver by 7.

Bam Bam Cam (6–4) vs. The Trading Block (7–3)

Nick's got a problem. His last three scores have gone from 155 to 135 to 117, which is the sort of trend that usually gets somebody called into the editor's office around here.

He's also got Dalton Kincaid listed as doubtful in his starting lineup. Fortunately, Deebo Samuel is available on the bench, and Patrick Mahomes is sitting behind Josh Allen after scoring nearly 39 points last week.

A difficult decision, having two good quarterbacks.

I've never had that problem. Most of the time I'm lucky if the road crew has two functioning radios.

Paul's got problems of his own. Smith-Njigba is on bye, Walker's unavailable, and Omar Cooper is currently selected despite being ruled out.

Lakota suggested I mention that managers should check their lineups before Sunday.

I told her that sounded suspiciously like useful advice and asked whether it was consistent with the newspaper's editorial standards.

She said she couldn't find any editorial standards.

I told her that's why I like working here.

She said she'd figured that out when she saw the editor's office had an open-door policy but no actual door.

This one should be competitive, but Nick has more available talent and several straightforward opportunities to improve his lineup.

The Pylon's Line: Nick by 12.

Quack Quack (5–5) vs. Big Apple (5–5)

Two brothers. Identical records. Both fighting to stay within reach of the playoffs.

And both coming off ridiculous performances.

Ken scored 206 points last week. Kyle scored 185. Between them, that's nearly 400 fantasy points, which is more production than I've seen from the entire Department of Transportation since 1998.

Ken's got McCaffrey, Purdy, Henry and enough running backs to make the decision difficult. Kyle's got Gibbs, Breece Hall and Trevor Lawrence, although his lineup's taken a beating from bye weeks.

Kyle has already banked fifteen points from Houston's defense. Not a bad start.

I asked Lakota who she liked in this one. She said both teams had legitimate strengths and that we should evaluate the available players, recent scoring trends and injury reports before making a decision.

Very thorough. Very professional.

I asked if she was planning to stand beside a highway all afternoon thinking about it.

She said no, but judging by my prediction record, somebody probably should.

Had to give her that one.

Ken's coming off the highest score of last week, and I trust his running backs a little more than Kyle's patched-together lineup.

The Pylon's Line: Ken by 5.

GAME OF THE WEEK

Boot Tuten Boogie (6–4) vs. Giant Expectations (8–2)

Now here's an uncomfortable situation.

Grant is playing Lakota. Grant has won four of his last five, knocked off defending champion Paul last week, and moved into third place. Lakota's leading the league at 8–2, although Kyle finally put an end to her seven-game winning streak.

And Lakota, as of Friday morning, is apparently responsible for improving my professional performance.

I asked the editor whether this constituted a conflict of interest.

He said he didn't see why.

I asked whether I was expected to pick Lakota because she's working for the newspaper.

He said absolutely not.

I asked whether picking against her would affect my Performance Improvement Plan.

He said we'd have to review that.

Lakota overheard the conversation and said she'd be happy to put in writing that my prediction wouldn't affect her assessment.

I told her I appreciated that.

She said she couldn't guarantee it wouldn't affect the editor's assessment, because he appeared to be making up the rules as he went along.

Fair point.

Anyway, Grant's already had a rough Thursday. Jonathan Taylor scored 3.70, and his kicker added another three. Six-point-seven from two positions, which is about what you'd expect from a snowplow operator trying to run a lemonade stand in February.

Grant's also got bigger problems than his fantasy lineup. Daniel Jones was released by the Colts, and there's talk the Patriots might sign him.

Grant's terrified New England is about to reunite Jones and Tommy DeVito.

I told him I'd seen that quarterback combination before. Wouldn't wish it on anybody.

Lakota reminded me that nothing had been confirmed.

I told her neither had Grant's recovery.

But he's got some weapons left. Pickens scored more than forty last week, Emmett Johnson's getting an opportunity with Walker unavailable, and the Chicago players are returning from their bye.

Lakota has Cook, Irving, Lamb and Higgins, along with enough depth to make life difficult for anybody. She's also got Kirk Cousins starting in place of Stafford, which could be interesting.

I explained that I intended to make this selection entirely on football merit, regardless of our new professional relationship.

She agreed, said that was exactly how it should be, and reminded me that transparent, evidence-based decisions were among Pierre's recommendations.

I told her I preferred the old system, where I made a guess and blamed the weather if it didn't work.

She said the old system was what got me suspended.

I've studied the numbers, considered the matchups, and consulted my clipboard. Grant's early deficit concerns me, and while Lakota's coming off a loss, I don't see her staying down for long.

The Pylon's Line: Lakota by 8.

Lakota reviewed the prediction and said it seemed reasonable.

I asked whether that meant my performance was improving.

She said we'd need a larger sample size.

I asked how large.

She said she'd let me know when I started getting the picks wrong again.

Apparently I'm working for a lawyer already.

FROM THE EDITOR'S DESK

Management is pleased to confirm that Gordie MacNeil has returned to his regular duties and that our new reader-engagement initiative is progressing satisfactorily.

Ms. Lakota has already identified several opportunities to improve editorial consistency, personnel documentation and communications procedures.

Mr. MacNeil has been asked to begin using electronic documents rather than handwritten notes on his clipboard.

He has agreed to consider the proposal once someone explains where the documents go when the electricity is off.

We are also pleased to report that Ms. Lakota has offered to assist with the newspaper's ongoing legal and administrative matters.

Management believes this will be beneficial.

Mr. MacNeil has requested that she review his compensation agreement first.

Ms. Lakota has now requested the editor's compensation agreement as well.

Management is reconsidering the entire initiative.

Gordie's previous official record: Week 9 — 4–1 outright, 4–1 against the lines. Week 10 predictions were supplied by a temporary guest columnist whose appointment has since concluded.
