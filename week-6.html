THE PYLON’S LINES: WEEK 6

October 17, 2026 · Gordie “The Pylon” MacNeil
Senior Road Crew Stop/Slow Technician

Right. Late again. Paul asked for my qualifications. I put down “traffic management.” He wanted to know how that helped me assess a tight end.

I said I’d spent years telling large men where to stand.

He requested references.

Thursday’s football has meanwhile finished. These are my picks from where we stand Saturday. Anything already scored is evidence, not foresight. Apparently that distinction matters now.

The Pylon’s Matchup of the Week

Big Apple (Ken, 3–2) vs. The Trading Block (Paul, 4–1)

My line: The Trading Block -9½
My winner: The Trading Block

Paul’s conducting interviews before accepting any more fantasy advice. Wants credentials, three references and an explanation for Alvin Kamara’s point-six. One analyst said he trusted the process. Paul asked whether the process was available for questioning.

The three Saints supplied less than fourteen points between them last week. Paul dropped Kamara for Darius Cooper, then replaced Cooper with Pat Freiermuth. The point-six has been released without a reference.

This is an awkward week to introduce professional standards, because Ken insists he does no research and still arrives with McCaffrey, Henry and Isaiah Likely. Paul wants to see everybody’s working. Ken maintains there wasn’t any.

Ken’s shopping this week was a free-agent defense: out went Green Bay, in came the Rams. Green Bay had just scored twenty-two for him. I’d have thought that bought another week, but apparently the warranty expired Tuesday.

“At that price, you can’t leave it there.”

Paul, meanwhile, already has more than twenty-five points from Smith-Njigba on Thursday. That’s a proper head start. Walker and McBride are still coming, and Bryce Young takes the quarterback job with Hurts out. Olave is questionable, but he and Vele remain selected; the New Orleans office has been reduced rather than closed.

Purdy and McCaffrey give Ken a substantial Monday reply. I wouldn’t be comfortable watching them with a small lead. I’m taking Paul by nine and a half, which is just enough room to remain publicly confident and privately check the score.

“Based on what?”

I turned the clipboard over. There are limits to an interview.

Quack Quack (Kyle, 3–2) vs. Boot Tuten Boogie (Grant, 2–3)

My line: Boot Tuten Boogie -6½
My winner: Boot Tuten Boogie

Kyle scored 211.65 last week. I’ve included the exact figure because I expect he’ll be checking.

Gibbs contributed nearly thirty-seven points and has now taken his bye. Kyle’s acceptance speech appears to have a longer season.

Thursday brought Waddle’s 5.40 against Emanuel Wilson’s 5.20 for Grant. Kyle leads by twenty cents. He’ll tell you it’s momentum.

Grant won by seventeen last week after I asked for seventeen and a half. I don’t mind being wrong. I object to being wrong by an amount you could lose down the sofa.

Still, I’m backing him again. Taylor gets Tennessee, Swift gets Atlanta, and Pickens and Watson give Grant an interest on both sides of Sunday night’s Dallas–Green Bay game. Taylor and Swift remain on the bill; Emanuel Wilson has already finished a fairly short opening set.

Grant’s spent twenty-six dollars on Germie Bernard. Ken would like to know whether he checked Harrisons first.

Kyle has Daniels, Bowers and London to argue with that. He also has the forty-two-dollar Braelon Allen, who is becoming rather more useful with Hall out and Gibbs unavailable. A good week here and Kyle may start describing the price as foresight. I remember the purchase. I’m withholding that word.

Grant needs Caleb Williams’s questionable hamstring to cooperate, and both teams have Monday injury decisions still to settle. I prefer Grant’s remaining group by six and a half. Kyle can bring last week’s total if he likes, but he’ll have to carry it himself.

Giant Expectations (Lakota, 4–1) vs. Dartastic (Sarah, 0–5)

My line: Giant Expectations -21½
My winner: Giant Expectations

Sarah fed the cottage, won Thanksgiving and put up her season’s best fantasy score. Her 155.25 could have beaten five other teams. The league has thanked her by sending Lakota next.

Doesn’t do cards, this organization. Just fixtures.

Lakota is four-and-one after ending Paul’s unbeaten run in front of an appreciative cottage. Stafford and Kyren get Arizona this week, Cook gets Las Vegas, and Lamb is still there to make a perfectly reasonable opponent feel badly outnumbered. I called them Giant Expectations, not Giant Exaggerations. I’m leaving that assessment where it is.

Sarah has brought in Aaron Rodgers and Brian Thomas Jr., both currently starting. Thomas has travelled from Ken’s discarded receivers to Sarah’s lineup. I expect Ken will be delighted if she finds a use for him, provided nobody asks why he didn’t.

Barkley and Rice remain out, and Evans, Tyler Warren and Dowdle still need their availability watched. That is an unreasonable amount of administration for somebody who has already done the dinner.

Sarah has replaced Bates for Detroit’s bye, with Tyler Bass now kicking. That sorts the kicker. I’m less sure anyone can fix her schedule.

Lakota by twenty-one and a half. I would still rather be invited to Sarah’s.

Sleezy Sandbars (Oliver, 3–2) vs. Bam Bam Cam (Nick, 3–2)

My line: Bam Bam Cam -21½
My winner: Bam Bam Cam

I’ve read the paper’s Coleman–Moore inquiry. Nick traded active Coleman to his fiancée for inactive Moore before Monday’s kickoff. Neither winner changed. All that paperwork to establish that the standings had been left exactly where they were.

This week Moore is actually in Nick’s starting lineup, although his shoulder leaves him questionable. Coleman is still starting for Lakota. We may finally get some football to go with the documentation.

Oliver has the early lead after Dobbins and Seattle’s defense supplied 15.60 Thursday; Nick has eight from Myers. Oliver has now moved Burrow and Chase out while Cincinnati is on bye.

Jordan Love and Xavier Worthy have come off his bench to replace them. Oliver recruits scientists for a living. He has located Jordan Love without commissioning a study.

I’m giving him credit for sorting those byes. Jeanty, Nabers, Adams and Kittle can still make a proper matchup of it. But Nick has Allen and Puka waiting, plus Jaylen Warren after last week’s nearly thirty-four points. That’s quite a bit to meet while rebuilding the front of your lineup.

Nick by twenty-one and a half. Oliver’s three wins have already exceeded last season’s two; I’d advise against treating that as permission to take Cincinnati’s holiday with them.

Drake & Bake (Carlie, 2–3) vs. I Love Pitts (Luc, 1–4)

My line: Drake & Bake -31½
My winner: Drake & Bake

I Love Pitts finally has Pitts starting. The name and the lineup have opened diplomatic relations.

After Pitts scored more than fifteen on the bench while Hockenson supplied less than two, Luc dropped Hockenson to add Ka’imi Fairbairn. He’s replaced a tight end with somebody paid to kick things. I understand the mood.

Unfortunately, Amon-Ra and Jameson Williams are on bye, and Jeremiyah Love is still in a flex slot despite being ruled out. Allgeier and DeVonta Smith are available on the bench. There is help to bring in; I’m not declaring the Saturday selections permanent.

Carlie has already collected 31.60 from Harvey and Denver’s defense on Thursday. Maye, Bijan and Nico Collins haven’t started. That’s a considerable queue to discover outside your door while you’re still rearranging the furniture.

I backed Carlie last week and Nick beat her by forty-nine. I’ve reviewed the problem and determined it was Nick. Fortunately, she has Luc this time.

I’m laying thirty-one and a half, even allowing for Luc to replace the unavailable Love. I admire the reconciliation with Pitts. I don’t think Carlie needs to postpone anything for it.

That’s Paul, Grant, Lakota, Nick and Carlie on the clipboard.

Paul still wants references. I’ve given him the foreman’s number. If he gets him on the phone, he’s already achieved more than the crew.
