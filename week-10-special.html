THE SHORE REPORT: THE BYE WEEK NOBODY AUTHORIZED

Two Missing Columns. One Missing Sportswriter. And Cam Skattebo Has a Plan.

Week 10 Saturday Night Special · November 14, 2026
Guest Columnist: Cam Skattebo (Fictional Parody)

Editor's note: This article is fictional satire. Cam Skattebo did not write, participate in, or endorse this column. All guest dialogue and newsroom events are invented.

FROM THE EDITOR'S DESK

The Shore Report wishes to address concerns regarding the absence of our Week 9 Tuesday Reaction and the failure to publish this week's Pylon Picks by their usual Thursday deadline.

Following an internal performance review, our regular columnist, Gordie "The Pylon" MacNeil, was placed on temporary administrative leave. Unfortunately, due to what management is describing as an "unanticipated staffing oversight," nobody was assigned to replace him.

We would like to emphasize that Mr. MacNeil's leave was unrelated to his football predictions, which, upon further examination, were correct in four of five matchups last week.

Management was unaware of this development when the decision was made.

In an effort to restore confidence in our publication, we have secured the services of New York Giants running back Cam Skattebo, whose recent experience with being benched made him uniquely qualified to understand Mr. MacNeil's situation.

Mr. Skattebo was provided with our editorial guidelines, a laptop, and access to the office.

The laptop has been recovered.

The office door has not.

CAM SKATTEBO: I DON'T DO BYE WEEKS

When these newspaper guys called and asked me to write their football column, I thought somebody was messing with me. I've never written for a newspaper before, but they said they needed somebody who understood football, somebody who wasn't afraid to make tough decisions, and somebody who could meet a deadline.

I asked when the games started. They said Sunday.

I asked what day it was. They said Saturday.

So what's the problem?

Apparently their regular writer was supposed to submit something Thursday, and everybody's been running around acting like the season's over. In football, if you've got time on the clock, you've got time to make a play. These newspaper guys have a whole day left and they're already waving the white flag.

First thing they did was hand me fourteen pages of editorial procedures. I told them I don't generally read the entire playbook either. Just tell me where we're going, who's trying to stop us, and let me handle the rest.

The editor pointed toward his office and said we'd discuss the process inside.

I went through the door.

Apparently he wanted me to open it first.

THE GORDIE SITUATION

Now, I met this Gordie guy, and I've got to tell you, I like him. He's been standing beside a highway in Nova Scotia for something like 23 years, holding a sign that tells people when to stop and when to move.

I asked him why the sign said STOP on one side and SLOW on the other. Those are the two worst words in football. I told him we were getting him a new sign that said RUN on one side and RUN HARD on the other.

He said that would cause a six-car pileup outside Truro.

I told him you can't worry about the secondary before you've established the running game.

Anyway, Gordie's apparently on some kind of Performance Improvement Plan. They want him to be more accurate, improve his attitude, and engage with readers. I asked what his record was last week.

Four wins. One loss.

FOUR AND ONE!

They benched a guy who went four and one? I know a little something about getting benched. Happened to me after a fumble. You make a mistake, you learn from it, and the next chance you get, you go out and run for 124 yards.

Gordie made one wrong prediction and they sent him home.

I told the editor that if he wanted better performance, maybe he should start by reviewing his own decisions.

He said I wasn't authorized to conduct performance reviews.

I told him I wasn't authorized to run through his door either, but here we are.

WEEK 9: LET'S TALK ABOUT WHAT EVERYBODY MISSED

First thing I noticed looking at last week's results is that Ken beat Carlie by six-tenths of a point. 150.55 to 149.95.

Six-tenths!

Carlie scored almost 150 points, which would have beaten six other teams in the league, and somehow she lost. Then I find out she had C.J. Stroud sitting on her bench with 39.20 points.

I don't understand fantasy benches. You've got a guy who can put up 39 points, and you're paying him to watch? In my business, if somebody's producing like that, you get him the ball until somebody physically removes him from the stadium.

Then there's Lakota, who beat Oliver 150.30 to 119.25. Seven straight wins, eight and one overall, and she doesn't seem particularly interested in letting anybody else have a turn.

I respect that.

Paul put up the week's highest score, 154.90, against Kyle. Nick beat his own mother by almost 39 points, which seems a little excessive to me. My mom would've made me apologize before I got dessert.

But the thing that really got my attention happened Thursday night, right before I agreed to write this article.

I played Washington. Eighteen carries, 78 yards, two touchdowns, and another 44 yards receiving. Pretty good night. That's 26.20 fantasy points in this league.

So I asked which fantasy manager had me.

They said Sarah.

I asked how many points I scored for her.

They said zero.

I figured there was a computer problem. Maybe the scoring system was broken, maybe somebody forgot to enter my touchdowns. Turns out Sarah had me on her BENCH.

TWENTY-SIX POINTS ON THE BENCH!

Now I'm told Sarah's a devoted Giants fan, and I appreciate the support. But I'm starting to understand why Gordie needs a lawyer. You people have some very unusual ideas about personnel management.

Sarah, no hard feelings. Seriously. But next time I score two touchdowns, I'd appreciate it if you at least let me into the building.

WEEK 10: CAM'S FIVE GAMES TO RUN THROUGH

The editor says I have to pick five winners. I told him that was fine, but I wasn't doing any of those fancy Pylon lines because Gordie's got a patent on those or something.

So these are my picks. I've looked at the matchups, I've checked the rosters, and I've decided which direction we're going.

Anybody standing in the way is welcome to try stopping us.

Drake & Bake (2–7) vs. Dartastic (1–8)

Carlie almost scored 150 last week and lost by less than a point. That's the kind of loss that sticks with you, especially when you've got enough unused points on the bench to change the outcome.

She's got Drake Maye, Bijan Robinson, Chase Brown and Nico Collins, and I think she's going to come out looking to make somebody pay for last week's disappointment.

Sarah, meanwhile, has already left my 26.20 points on the bench, which I believe we've covered. Her Giants defense gave her six, so she's got a head start, but I'm having some trouble understanding her overall strategy.

I called her about it. She said fantasy football isn't quite the same as real football.

That's what Gordie told me, too.

I'm beginning to think fantasy football might be the problem.

Cam's Call: Carlie by 18.

Bam Bam Cam (6–3) vs. I Love Pitts (2–7)

Now we're talking. Finally, somebody in this league understands what football is all about.

Nick's team is called Bam Bam Cam. Named after me. Apparently he's been running around with my name on his franchise all season, and I didn't even know about it until these newspaper guys handed me the matchup sheet.

I asked if he had permission to use my name. The editor said fantasy teams don't generally need licensing agreements. I told him we'd discuss that after I finished reviewing the endorsement opportunities.

But I like Nick already. He's 6–3, sitting in third place, and clearly recognizes talent when he sees it. Unlike certain other members of his family who apparently believe 26 fantasy points belong on a bench.

I'm not naming names, Sarah. We've already talked about this.

Now here's the strange part. Nick doesn't even have me on his roster. His mother does! She owns the actual Cam Skattebo, leaves me sitting on the bench, and meanwhile her son names his entire organization after me.

I don't know what kind of family business they're running here, but somebody needs to get everybody in the same room and sort out the paperwork.

Anyway, Nick's got Josh Allen, Justin Jefferson, Javonte Williams and enough tight ends to start a construction company. Puka's out, which hurts, but there's still plenty of talent on that roster.

Luc's team is called I Love Pitts because he's got a running back named Love and a tight end named Pitts. I respect the creativity, but Love's been injured and Pitts hasn't exactly been carrying the organization.

That's a tough business model. Imagine naming your restaurant after two chefs and finding out one's on vacation while the other burns the toast.

Luc's got some talent, and I like a guy who sticks with his people. But there's loyalty, and then there's watching the scoreboard while your franchise motto slowly becomes a cry for help.

And before anybody accuses me of favoritism, let me make something clear. I reviewed this matchup objectively. I looked at both rosters, considered the injuries, studied the recent results, and reached a completely impartial conclusion.

Cam's Call: Nick by 15.

Anybody who names a football team after me deserves at least fifteen points. That's just good sportsmanship.

Big Apple (4–5) vs. Sleezy Sandbars (6–3)

Ken's playing his son Oliver, and this one interests me because Ken's already got a lead. Isaiah Likely put up 26.70 points Thursday, while Oliver got 4.40 out of Malik Nabers.

Twenty-five points is a pretty nice head start, especially when Oliver's running back situation looks like somebody raided the equipment room and took all the good helmets.

But Oliver's got Burrow and Chase, and those two can make a lot of points appear in a hurry. I saw what they did the week before. You don't ignore that kind of firepower just because you're down early.

Ken also has a player listed as out in his starting lineup. I'm told he can replace him before kickoff, which is good, because playing somebody who isn't available seems like a terrible way to win a football game.

I'm learning a lot about this league. Most of it concerns me.

Cam's Call: Ken by 10.

Quack Quack (4–5) vs. Giant Expectations (8–1)

Kyle's got Gibbs, Bowers, Hall and enough potential to beat just about anybody. He's also got a habit of leaving productive players on his bench, which I now understand is a family-wide recreational activity around here.

Lakota's won seven straight, and she's sitting at the top of the league like she owns the place. She's got Cook, Kyren Williams, Lamb and Higgins, and she keeps putting up around 150 points without making much noise about it.

I don't trust people who win that quietly. If I won seven straight, I'd be telling everybody. I'd put it on my truck. I'd have shirts made.

Kyle nearly beat her in Week 1. Lost by 1.90 points, which means this might be closer than the records suggest.

But I'm not picking against somebody who's won seven in a row just because the other guy has a duck for a logo.

Cam's Call: Lakota by 6.

GAME OF THE WEEK

Boot Tuten Boogie (5–4) vs. The Trading Block (7–2)

Now THIS is football.

Grant and Paul played for the championship last year. Paul won. They met again in Week 1, scored almost 400 points between them, and Paul won by a single point.

ONE POINT!

That's not a football game. That's two guys trying to shove each other through the same doorway, which, as I've recently learned, can get expensive.

Paul's sitting second in the league and has scored more total points than anybody. Grant's in fifth, one win outside the playoff positions, and he's got every reason to want this one.

But here's where it gets interesting.

Paul has Kenneth Walker. Great running back. Scored 35.30 points last week. Unfortunately, he's out this weekend.

And who benefits from Walker being out?

Emmett Johnson.

Who has Emmett Johnson?

Grant.

So Paul's running back gets hurt, and Grant's running back gets a bigger opportunity. I don't know who writes these scripts, but somebody deserves a raise.

Grant's also got 18.30 points from Stefon Diggs already in the bank. Paul hasn't scored yet. That's a real advantage, although Paul's got enough talent to make up the difference.

I went through this matchup three times, which the editor says is more research than Gordie usually does in a month. I told him that seemed unfair to Gordie, considering the guy was four and one last week.

The editor asked if I was here to write football predictions or represent his suspended employee.

I told him I could do both.

He said that would be a conflict of interest.

I asked if he wanted to discuss it outside.

Anyway, I'm taking Grant. He's got the head start, he's got the motivation, and he's got a chance to knock off the guy who beat him in the championship.

Cam's Call: Grant by 3.

If I'm wrong, fine. We'll line up again next week and run the same play harder.

That's how you solve problems.

FINAL THOUGHTS FROM CAM

I've enjoyed writing for The Shore Report, although I still don't understand why newspapers have so many rules. You want football predictions? Ask a football player. You want somebody to sit in a chair and fill out forms? Hire an accountant.

As for Gordie, I think he's getting a raw deal. The guy went four and one, and instead of giving him a raise, they made him sign paperwork. I've decided I'm going to represent him at his disciplinary hearing.

The editor says I'm not qualified.

I told him neither is he, judging by the state of his office door.

We're going to get Gordie his job back. We're going to get these columns published on time. And if anybody tries to stop us, well, I've got a pretty good idea how we're going to handle that.

Gordie says that's exactly what he's afraid of.

FROM THE EDITOR'S DESK — FINAL NOTICE

The Shore Report thanks Mr. Skattebo for his contribution to this week's emergency edition. While his enthusiasm for the assignment was appreciated, several administrative matters remain unresolved, including reimbursement for damages to newspaper property.

Mr. Skattebo has formally offered to represent Gordie MacNeil during his upcoming disciplinary hearing.

Mr. MacNeil has declined the offer and requested legal counsel instead.

We anticipate Mr. MacNeil's return for Week 11, subject to the successful completion of his Performance Improvement Plan. Management has also begun reviewing opportunities to provide additional assistance with his reader-engagement objectives.

Mr. Skattebo has been informed that his guest appointment has concluded and that his presence will not be required at future editorial meetings.

He has indicated that he intends to attend anyway.

Security has been advised.

Unfortunately, the security office also has a door.
