THE SHORE REPORT HAS RETAINED LEGAL COUNSEL

Week 10 Tuesday Reaction — An Independent Investigation into Missing Columns, Mismanaged Rosters, and One Very Expensive Office Door

November 17, 2026
Special Investigator: Pierre Poilievre (Fictional Parody)

Editor's note: This article is fictional satire. Pierre Poilievre did not write, participate in, or endorse this investigation. All dialogue and newsroom events are invented.

FROM THE EDITOR'S DESK

Following Saturday's unfortunate incident involving Cam Skattebo and the structural integrity of our offices, The Shore Report has retained outside assistance to resolve our ongoing personnel dispute with Gordie MacNeil.

We are pleased to welcome Pierre Poilievre, whose more than two decades of parliamentary experience make him, in management's opinion, exceptionally qualified to investigate employment matters.

Mr. Poilievre is not a licensed attorney. Management became aware of this distinction after extending the appointment.

We remain confident in our decision.

AN APPLE, A BRIEFCASE, AND QUESTION PERIOD

Pierre Poilievre arrived Tuesday morning carrying a briefcase, a red apple, and a list of questions. The editor welcomed him, expressed confidence that the matter could be resolved quickly, and began explaining the circumstances surrounding Gordie MacNeil's suspension.

Pierre asked why the front door was missing.

The editor explained that an NFL running back had recently been retained to assist with the newspaper's football coverage and that certain structural modifications had occurred during his visit.

Pierre took a bite of his apple.

"So you suspended a columnist who correctly predicted four out of five matchups, replaced him with a football player who demolished your office, and missed two publication deadlines?"

The editor explained that this was a considerable oversimplification of a complex personnel matter.

"Which part is incorrect?"

The editor consulted his notes.

Pierre had spent more than twenty years asking uncomfortable questions in Parliament. He'd encountered evasive ministers, lengthy explanations and people who could speak for fifteen minutes without answering anything. But this was impressive, even by Ottawa standards.

The editor suggested that Pierre might not fully understand the newspaper business.

Pierre explained that he'd delivered the Calgary Sun as a boy and therefore had considerable experience in newspaper distribution.

"Delivering newspapers isn't the same as publishing them," the editor replied.

"How many newspapers have you delivered this week?"

"None."

"And now we've identified the problem."

The editor requested that they move on to the football results.

Pierre agreed. He'd brought another apple.

EXHIBIT A: FIVE MATCHUPS. FIVE UPSETS. ZERO ACCOUNTABILITY.

The first document presented to the investigator was the Week 10 scoreboard.

Pierre studied it carefully before asking whether it had been printed upside down.

Every team entering the week with the worse record had won. All four teams occupying playoff positions had lost. The league's longest winning streak was over, and the highest-scoring manager in the standings had barely broken 100 points.

"Mr. Editor, you have ten teams, five matchups, and every single favorite has failed to deliver?"

"Fantasy football is unpredictable."

"Then why are you evaluating Gordie on his ability to predict it?"

The editor requested a short recess.

Pierre declined.

SARAH WINS. CAM SKATTEBO'S GRIEVANCE IS DISMISSED.

Dartastic 130.95 — Drake & Bake 126.00

Sarah won her second matchup in three weeks despite leaving Saturday's guest columnist, Cam Skattebo, and his 26.20 points on the bench.

Pierre examined the evidence. Chuba Hubbard scored 33.20, Jared Goff added 23.45, and Sarah won by 4.95 points without Cam's assistance.

"So the employee complained about being benched, his replacement outperformed him, and the organization won anyway?"

"Correct."

"Then what exactly is his complaint?"

The editor explained that Skattebo believed he deserved an opportunity to contribute.

Pierre suggested he could make that argument after paying for the office door.

Carlie, meanwhile, received just 3.50 points from Drake Maye after an in-game injury. C.J. Stroud scored 22.55 on her bench, enough to have reversed the result.

"Could she have predicted the injury?"

"No."

"Then let's stop punishing people for failing to predict the unpredictable."

The editor glanced nervously toward Gordie's employment file.

NICK'S ENDORSEMENT DEAL COLLAPSES

I Love Pitts 145.95 — Bam Bam Cam 117.20

Nick named his entire franchise after Cam Skattebo. Cam was so delighted that he predicted Nick would beat Luc by fifteen points.

Luc won by nearly twenty-nine.

Dak Prescott delivered 37.85, Amon-Ra St. Brown added 25.80, and Luc recorded one of his strongest performances of the season.

Nick, meanwhile, had Patrick Mahomes and Deebo Samuel sitting on his bench with a combined 64.80 points. Two legal substitutions would have turned his defeat into a victory.

Pierre requested the endorsement agreement.

"There isn't one," the editor explained.

"So the franchise uses a professional football player's name, receives his public endorsement, and performs considerably worse than expected?"

"Yes."

"And nobody was paid?"

"No."

Pierre made a note.

"Finally. A transaction in this organization that demonstrates fiscal responsibility."

Nick falls to 6–4 and the final playoff position. Luc improves to 3–7, apparently having discovered that you can win football games without naming your franchise after someone famous.

KEN'S COMPLETE LACK OF RESEARCH PRODUCES RECORD RESULTS

Big Apple 206.25 — Sleezy Sandbars 148.75

Ken, whose preparation methods have long been described as suspiciously relaxed, produced the highest score of Week 10.

Christian McCaffrey scored 46.30, Brock Purdy added 34.55, Isaiah Likely contributed 26.70, and the Rams defense supplied another 21. Even more remarkably, Ken had selected the highest-scoring legal lineup available from his captured roster.

Oliver managed a respectable 148.75 and still lost to his own father by 57.50 points.

Pierre requested Ken's research methodology.

The editor explained that Ken doesn't generally conduct much research.

"So the man who does the least research produces the most points?"

"Apparently."

"And he selected the optimal lineup?"

"Yes."

Pierre looked around at the editor, the paperwork, and the missing door.

"How many people are employed here to analyze fantasy football?"

The editor declined to answer.

Pierre recommended immediately reducing the research budget.

GRANT DEFEATS PAUL. THE CHAMPIONSHIP AUDIT BEGINS.

Boot Tuten Boogie 150.25 — The Trading Block 100.50

Grant finally got revenge against Paul, who beat him in last year's championship and again by a single point in Week 1.

George Pickens scored 42.50, Emmett Johnson added 33.10, and Grant won by almost fifty points.

Paul, the league's leading scorer entering the week, managed just 100.50. He also left Minnesota's 28-point defensive performance on his bench.

"Would starting that defense have changed the outcome?" Pierre asked.

"No."

"Then why are we discussing it?"

"Because it was a questionable decision."

"Mr. Editor, the man lost by fifty points. He doesn't need another defense. He needs another investigation."

Paul reportedly responded with his usual question: "Based on what?"

Pierre was delighted.

Finally, somebody who understood the importance of demanding evidence.

KYLE ENDS LAKOTA'S STREAK. THE INVESTIGATOR REQUESTS A RECOUNT.

Quack Quack 185.50 — Giant Expectations 120.45

Lakota entered the week at 8–1, having won seven straight. Kyle entered at 4–5, desperately trying to stay in the playoff conversation.

Kyle won by 65.05 points.

Gibbs scored 41.20, Trevor Lawrence added 30.30, Parker Washington delivered 26.20, and kicker Cam Little contributed another 19.

Pierre studied the numbers.

"So the sixth-place team has just defeated the league leader by sixty-five points?"

"Yes."

"And the sixth-place team has scored more points this season than eight of the other nine teams?"

"Yes."

"Then why is he sixth?"

The editor began explaining head-to-head scheduling, accumulated records, and the league's playoff structure.

Pierre interrupted.

"Sounds like a system designed by the same people who suspended Gordie."

EXHIBIT B: THE SKATTEBO EXPERIMENT

Having completed his review, Pierre requested the performance evaluation of the newspaper's temporary replacement.

Cam Skattebo had predicted five winners in Saturday's emergency edition.

He got two right.

Pierre compared this with Gordie's previous performance: four correct predictions out of five, including four successful lines.

"So you suspended an eighty-percent performer and replaced him with a forty-percent performer?"

The editor explained that Cam had been hired for his football expertise and ability to generate reader engagement.

"And the results?"

The editor glanced toward the missing door.

"Engagement has increased significantly."

Pierre asked whether that included the insurance adjuster, the building contractor, and the police officer who'd stopped by Monday.

The editor requested another recess.

EXHIBIT C: THE PLAYOFF RACE

With four regular-season matchups remaining, Lakota leads at 8–2, followed by Paul at 7–3. Grant has moved into third at 6–4, Nick holds fourth, and Oliver sits just outside the playoff positions with the same record.

Kyle and Ken are both 5–5, close enough to make the final month uncomfortable for everyone above them.

Pierre was particularly interested in the fact that every team occupying a playoff position entering Week 10 had lost.

"An extraordinary lack of accountability at the top," he observed.

The editor explained that losing a fantasy matchup was not a disciplinary offense.

"Then why was Gordie suspended after winning four predictions out of five?"

The editor had no response.

Pierre finished his apple, shook his head thoughtfully, and said, 'It was such a good apple.'

Nobody was entirely sure whether he was referring to the fruit or the investigation.

THE INVESTIGATOR'S FINDINGS

After several hours reviewing football results, personnel files, financial records that did not exist, and the remains of the office entrance, Pierre delivered his preliminary recommendations.

Gordie MacNeil should be reinstated immediately. The newspaper should discontinue its practice of hiring professional athletes without first checking whether they understand how doors operate. And all future employment decisions should be supported by actual evidence rather than the editor's personal dissatisfaction.

The editor asked whether Pierre could summarize his recommendations into something suitable for publication.

Pierre smiled. He'd been waiting for this.

FIX THE PICKS. FIRE THE EDITOR. FREE GORDIE.

The editor looked up.

"Fire the editor? I thought you were here to defend management."

Pierre explained that he had been hired to conduct an independent investigation.

"But I'm the editor!"

"Then you're in an excellent position to implement the recommendation."

The editor asked whether there was any possibility of reconsidering.

Pierre explained that he understood losing a seat better than most people. He'd lost his own parliamentary seat in 2025 before winning another in Alberta.

"Sometimes," he said, "you have to lose your seat before you appreciate having one."

The editor asked whether he was suggesting a relocation.

Pierre assured him that Alberta was not currently part of the newspaper's restructuring plan.

FROM THE EDITOR'S DESK — FINAL NOTICE

The Shore Report thanks Mr. Poilievre for his assistance, although management wishes to clarify that he was retained to advise the newspaper, not replace its editorial leadership.

His recommendations remain under review.

Gordie MacNeil's administrative leave may conclude before Thursday's Week 11 predictions. Management has also contacted a consultant to assist with his reader-engagement objectives.

Mr. Poilievre has requested the consultant's qualifications, Gordie has requested legal representation, and Mr. Skattebo has offered to conduct the interview.

Management has declined all three requests.

Mr. Poilievre has submitted his final invoice. It includes six hours of consulting, travel expenses, and twelve apples.

The editor has disputed the apple charges.

Pierre has requested Question Period.

The office still doesn't have a door.
