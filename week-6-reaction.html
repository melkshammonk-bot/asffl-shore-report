Oliver has the wins. Haleigh has the receipts.

The Sleezy Sandbars have introduced a weekly front-office reunion. Oliver remains general manager. His explanations now require supporting material.

The Shore Report has reviewed Haleigh’s qualifications and concluded that Oliver may have been underusing his front office. Years of Bravo have prepared the unofficial assistant GM to recognize shaky alliances, questionable deals, inflated confidence and people explaining that their original decision was actually a different decision.

She appears unusually well trained for this league.

Under the new arrangements, Haleigh has added Director of Vibes and Accountability to her responsibilities. Sources familiar with the seating plan say Oliver arrived hoping to discuss the future. Haleigh had screenshots of his preseason optimism. The future was asked to wait outside.

Fortunately, this week he also brought evidence worth keeping.

Sleezy Sandbars beat Bam Bam Cam 139.80–111.05, a 28.75-point win that takes Oliver to 4–2 and Nick to 3–3. Oliver has now doubled last season’s entire two-win return. His request to have that sentence read aloud at the reunion has been granted.

He earned Best Manager, selecting 100% of his best possible lineup, and Overachiever, finishing 18% above his 117.98 projection. Manager efficiency is a hindsight comparison after every score is known. This week, even the computer could not improve Oliver’s selections.

With Burrow and Chase on bye, Jordan Love supplied 23.00 and Xavier Worthy 12.30. Malik Nabers led the team with 26.50. The three-tight-end committee that produced 13.20 last week returned 38.70 through George Kittle, Colston Loveland and Jake Ferguson. The meeting has finally produced something besides minutes.

Haleigh’s James Kennedy–inspired victory playlist is ready, drawing on her appreciation of Vanderpump Rules. Oliver has learned that winning the matchup does not automatically confer control of the aux cord.

Nick’s bench requests a separate hearing

Nick received Worst Manager at 82% efficiency and Below Expectation, finishing 28% below his 153.57 projection. These are uncomfortable certificates to collect against a brother who has just completed the perfect lineup.

His 45.60 bench points led the league. DeMario Douglas supplied 17.60 without entering the lineup; Emeka Egbuka added 14.80 from the same department. MarShawn Lloyd, selected to play, contributed 1.00.

There were points available. There was not a hidden win. Nick’s best possible combination would have reached 135.65, still 4.15 short of Oliver. Josh Allen’s 20.85 and DK Metcalf’s 17.30 were useful, but the rest of the operation never supplied enough support.

The transactions desk has a separate item for the reunion. Nick dropped Deebo Samuel on Sunday while making room for Ryan Flournoy, briefly exchanged Flournoy for Cyrus Allen, then brought Flournoy back. Flournoy scored 14.00 in his lineup. On Wednesday, Nick paid $48 to reclaim Samuel.

The fourteen points counted on Sunday. The forty-eight dollars arrived afterward. Haleigh may wish to request the extended cut.

Paul produces references

Paul’s new insistence on credentials has coincided with a much more satisfactory set of answers.

The Trading Block beat Big Apple 165.65–121.00, winning The Pylon’s Matchup of the Week by 44.65. Paul returns to winning at 5–1; Ken falls to 3–3. Paul also collected Highest Scorer, his third weekly scoring title of the season.

Kenneth Walker supplied 27.90 and RB of the Week. Jaxon Smith-Njigba added 25.40, Bryce Young 22.65, and the Chiefs’ defense delivered 19.00 for DEF of the Week. Kansas City had cost Paul $1 on waivers.

That is a very affordable reference.

The New Orleans delegation was smaller and more productive: Chris Olave and Devaughn Vele combined for 20.50, comfortably exceeding the 13.80 last week’s three Saints supplied. The interview process may be working. Paul subsequently dropped Kansas City on Tuesday, suggesting that even a strong reference does not guarantee permanent employment.

Ken’s principal witnesses were Christian McCaffrey with 25.70, Isaiah Likely with 23.90, and Brock Purdy with 20.00. Likely retained TE of the Week, narrowly ahead of Kyle Pitts’s 23.00. Derrick Henry’s 3.00 left rather more work than usual for everybody else.

The bargain department did have something useful. Jakobi Meyers, Ken’s six-dollar purchase last week, scored 13.30 on the bench. This time the item worked; it simply had not been taken out of the box.

I Love Pitts discovers a practical application

Luc has put Pitts in the lineup and won. The newspaper records this development without attempting to make it more complicated.

I Love Pitts beat Drake & Bake 140.60–132.65, taking Narrow Victory by 7.95. Both managers are 2–4, although Luc has arrived there in a considerably better mood than last week’s awards cabinet suggested.

Pitts supplied 23.00 after scoring 15.20 on Luc’s bench a week earlier. Hockenson had been dropped for Ka’imi Fairbairn, who contributed 11.00. Affection and selection have finally attended the same meeting.

Bhayshul Tuten led Luc with 23.70, DeVonta Smith added 20.50, and Blake Corum supplied 13.00. Luc also moved Jeremiyah Love out of the lineup before the final selections. Last week’s Worst Manager reached 100% efficiency this time. Sleeper names Oliver Best Manager, but the arithmetic gives Luc an equal claim to a perfect lineup.

There may have to be two chairs at the sensible end of Haleigh’s reunion.

Carlie received another QB of the Week from Drake Maye, whose 26.15 edged the 26.10 C.J. Stroud scored on her bench. That decision was correct by five hundredths. Matthew Golden added 21.40, and Denver’s defense provided 18.00, one point behind Kansas City for the defensive award.

Bijan Robinson’s 5.30 and Nick Folk’s 1.00 made the afternoon harder. This was the week’s closest result.

Grant keeps the receipt. Kyle keeps last week’s score.

Boot Tuten Boogie beat Quack Quack 144.75–120.20, a 24.55-point win that leaves both at 3–3.

Grant’s $26 Germie Bernard purchase immediately supplied 20.70. Gordie had wondered whether he checked Harrisons first. The paper can confirm that the player was put to work promptly, which should satisfy the most important part of Ken’s inspection.

D’Andre Swift scored 23.00 and Jonathan Taylor 20.10. Taylor and Swift supplied 43.10 between them; this week the backfield earned the billing without requiring assistance from the dogs.

The result survived Juwan Johnson’s 0.00, with Brenton Strange’s 8.40 sitting on the bench and winning TE Benchwarmer of the Week. Grant had room to improve. Kyle did not have enough points to insist on it.

One week after 211.65, Kyle scored 91.45 fewer points. Gibbs was on bye, Jayden Daniels led the starters with 24.00, and free-agent addition Rachaad White supplied 15.70. The $42 Braelon Allen investment returned 12.40.

Trevor Lawrence’s 30.40 earned Kyle QB Benchwarmer of the Week, while Mike Washington’s 6.00 took RB Benchwarmer of the Week.

Kyle is welcome to bring last week’s score to the discussion. Haleigh has asked that screenshots include the date.

Lakota keeps winning. Sarah keeps getting the fixture list.

Giant Expectations beat Dartastic 151.90–101.55, extending Lakota’s winning streak to four and taking her to 5–1. The 50.35-point margin earned Biggest Blowout. Sarah is 0–6 and, with Lowest Scorer, has received another award she would cheerfully exchange for something edible.

Lakota’s leading contribution came from Tre Tucker: 26.70 and WR of the Week, just ahead of Nabers’s 26.50. James Cook supplied 24.30, while new kicker Chris Boswell returned 18.00 and K of the Week, one point ahead of Sarah’s Tyler Bass.

The Coleman–Moore paperwork has acquired a useful new attachment. Coleman scored 15.90 in Lakota’s lineup; Moore remained on Nick’s bench with 0.00. Remove Coleman’s points altogether and Lakota still wins by 34.45.

Giant Expectations is developing the useful habit of winning without requiring every previous newspaper inquiry to be resolved.

Sarah’s new arrivals did help. Aaron Rodgers scored 18.55, Bass 17.00, and Sunday addition Darnell Washington 12.50. Chuba Hubbard contributed 18.10, while Hunter Henry added 14.80. Cam Skattebo and Brian Thomas Jr. finished with zero, and the nonstarters offered only two points altogether. This was not a cupboard full of overlooked answers.

Through six weeks, Sarah has faced 992.85 points, the league’s highest total against any team. Last week she ran into the season’s first 200-point performance. This week she got a team on its fourth consecutive win.

Her Thanksgiving hospitality continues to be repaid through an unusually hostile booking system.

Elsewhere in the awards cabinet

This week’s report also issued K Benchwarmer of the Week to Luc’s Will Reichard with 0.00. Carlie’s nonstarter Evan McPherson also had zero. The distinction is administrative; neither supplied points the other manager missed.
