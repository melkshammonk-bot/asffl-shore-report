Grant will have time to think on his walk

Carlie beat Grant by exactly 50 points. That is a considerable amount of fantasy advice to return to sender.

Drake & Bake’s 165.90–115.90 win over Boot Tuten Boogie settled The Pylon’s Matchup of the Week with very little left for the household appeals process. Gordie had picked Carlie by 2½. He correctly identified the winner and approximately five per cent of the problem.

The Shore Report hereby sentences Grant to one week of walking the dogs, reduced from a month because he was a good sport. This is a newspaper ruling, issued under powers the newspaper has given itself.

The possibility that Grant let her win has been considered purely as a courtesy to Grant. Nico Collins scored 30.80, Bijan Robinson 26.70 and Zay Flowers 24.80. Carlie appears to have won without requiring his cooperation.

Nor did she need to get everything right. After being replaced by Sam Darnold, Drake Maye scored 24.85 on her bench, winning QB Benchwarmer of the Week and comfortably beating Darnold’s 15.00. Drake & Bake has discovered it can operate without the Drake, although the Drake has now asked management to reconsider.

Carlie also led the league with 73.15 bench points, ahead of Paul’s 69.35 and Nick’s 66.05. There was more available if Grant had made this difficult.

He did have legitimate trouble. Ladd McConkey left with a foot injury and no points, while Jonathan Taylor’s 21.70 led the starters who could help. Emanuel Wilson supplied 25.00 from the bench, but even Grant’s perfect lineup would have reached only 149.30. Carlie would still have won.

Grant finished as the week’s Lowest Scorer, dropping to 1–3, while Carlie returned to 2–2. Two weeks ago, the paper noted that finishing his exams would leave Grant more time to focus on Carlie’s team.

We may need to be more careful with our recommendations.

Kyle manages the defeat almost perfectly

Oliver has surpassed his entire win total from last season. For the third victory, he chose the uncle most qualified to explain why it should not have happened.

The Sleezy Sandbars beat Quack Quack 141.70–119.15, moving Oliver to 3–1 after a two-win 2025 season. He has now won three straight, which is beginning to interfere with several reliable jokes.

This one came despite Ja’Marr Chase leaving with a concussion after scoring 4.70. Joe Burrow delivered 27.00 and QB of the Week, Malik Nabers added 23.20, and George Kittle and Seattle’s defense supplied 15.00 each. Oliver had enough elsewhere to keep going.

He even left Kyle Monangai’s 27.00 on the bench, collecting RB Benchwarmer of the Week. Sarah’s Brian Robinson at 25.70 and Grant’s Emanuel Wilson at 25.00 were close behind. Oliver could have made better selections. He simply did not need to make them against Kyle.

Kyle, by contrast, made almost every available point count.

Sleeper awarded him Best Manager for reaching 98% of his best possible lineup. It also awarded him Below Expectation for finishing 27% below his 162.46 projection.

Kyle will presumably mention whichever certificate best suits the conversation.

The uncomfortable part is that both are fair. His optimal lineup was 122.15, only three points better than the one he selected and still 19.55 short of Oliver. There was no winning combination hiding on the bench. Kyle had arranged the available disappointment with considerable expertise.

Brock Bowers led him with 19.60. The $42 Braelon Allen pickup supplied 7.70, while Parker Washington and Josh Downs combined for 5.60.

Gordie called the Allen purchase a temporary access road. It has so far taken Kyle to 2–2.

Oliver, meanwhile, has moved beyond last season’s ceiling without using all of this season’s players. The family’s most experienced advice service may wish to let him continue unsupervised.

Luc has to stay in the chair for this

The Week 4 preview raised a specific difficulty for Luc: Dak Prescott could throw to CeeDee Lamb and improve both Luc’s afternoon and Lakota’s chances of beating him.

The difficulty has now been measured.

Dak scored 21.45 for Luc. Lamb scored 39.30 for Lakota. Under Gordie’s Chair Protocol, Luc was required to remain seated and experience both feelings. There is no obvious improvement available by moving to the other end of the couch.

Giant Expectations won 178.15–125.95, giving Lakota Highest Scorer, Biggest Blowout by 52.20, and a 3–1 record. She also collected Overachiever, finishing 22% above her 146.34 projection.

The quarterback choice was correct again. Matthew Stafford’s 12.85 beat Justin Herbert’s 10.45 by 2.40. After the opening weeks, getting that decision right deserves acknowledgment. It no longer requires a full inquiry.

Kyren Williams scored 34.70, Tee Higgins 25.70 and Sam LaPorta 21.40 alongside Lamb. Kyren won RB of the Week; LaPorta won TE of the Week, ahead of Bowers’s 19.60. Lakota could have started Herbert and still won by 49.80. The other players have relieved the quarterbacks of sole responsibility for family suspense.

Luc has responded to his earlier tight-end waiver debacle with a public declaration: his team is now officially I Love Pitts.

For once, Pitts offered some encouragement. His 7.70 beat the 1.90 Dalton Schultz supplied on Paul’s bench. The paper records this willingly. A man should be allowed to enjoy the week his discarded tight end does less damage from somebody else’s roster.

Luc’s kicker was more productive still. Will Reichard’s 19.00 won K of the Week, edging Sarah’s Jake Bates by one point.

Luc has changed the name. Lakota has changed the subject from whether she knows what she is doing to who might be able to stop her.

Ken could have stayed out of the shop

Last week, Ken beat Grant and acquired a receiver Grant had discarded. This week, he spent money of his own. The earlier arrangement was more successful.

His $27 Kalif Raymond pickup scored 1.60 as Big Apple lost 149.75–144.90 to Nick’s Bam Bam Cam. The 4.85-point escape earned Nick Narrow Victory in the week’s closest finish.

Ken already owned Brian Thomas Jr. He had drafted him. Thomas scored 9.70 on the bench.

Starting Thomas instead of Raymond, with everything else unchanged, would have given Ken 153.00 and a 3.25-point win. There was no further purchase required.

There was even a spare solution. Khalil Shakir’s 10.20 in Raymond’s place would have produced 153.50, enough to win by 3.75.

The paper has spent several weeks investigating whether Ken secretly does research. We may have overlooked the more pressing question of whether he checks the cupboard before going shopping.

Nick should be restrained in any advice he offers. Keon Coleman’s 22.60 won WR Benchwarmer of the Week, and DK Metcalf’s 16.50 also remained unused. Dalton Kincaid and Travis Kelce, both selected to play, combined for 5.20.

Fortunately, Javonte Williams scored 28.30 and Puka Nacua 27.70. Nick also chose correctly between his quarterbacks: Josh Allen’s 20.05 beat Patrick Mahomes’s 19.25.

Both managers are 2–2. Nick leaves with the win and several subjects best avoided. Ken leaves with the receipt.

Paul keeps winning. Sarah keeps losing players.

Paul reached 4–0 with a 173.65–132.30 win over Dartastic, powered by Tetairoa McMillan’s 43.20, the highest score from any starter in the league.

McMillan took WR of the Week ahead of Lamb’s 39.30. Kenneth Walker added 29.90, and Bryce Young’s 24.75 comfortably justified starting him over Jalen Hurts’s 13.45.

Gordie had observed that being unbeaten gave Paul room to make decisions the rest of the league would have to explain. Young has now provided the explanation. Paul can return to leaving trade offers where people will find them.

Sarah’s lineup had a much harsher afternoon. Saquon Barkley and Rashee Rice both suffered hamstring injuries during their games and finished with zero points. Both were injured after their games began, when Sarah could no longer replace them.

Dartastic still received 25.90 from Chuba Hubbard, 24.60 from Jared Goff and 18.00 from Jake Bates. The Giants’ defense scored 16.00, beating Seattle’s 15.00 for DEF of the Week. Sarah’s Giants loyalty did produce something the rest of the league was entitled to envy.

Sleeper nevertheless gave her Worst Manager, with 75% lineup efficiency. That measures the lineup against what would have been possible after every score was known, and the injuries during play heavily affected her result. The computer gets to choose its team on Tuesday.

Hunter Henry’s 11.20 earned Sarah TE Benchwarmer of the Week.

Sarah remains 0–4. Paul is still the only unbeaten manager, with 700.75 season points, leading Lakota’s 601.40 by 99.35. He even left Alvin Kamara’s 20.80 and $35 pickup Ollie Gordon’s 16.00 on the bench. Victory continues to excuse a fairly comfortable amount of inefficiency.

Gordie finished the week 4–1 on winners. All four correct favourites covered; Kyle was his only miss.

The clipboard was sound everywhere it placed less confidence in Kyle.

Elsewhere in the awards cabinet, Lakota’s Raiders defense earned DEF Benchwarmer of the Week with 4.00 points.

Paul leads at 4–0, followed by Lakota and Oliver at 3–1. Carlie, Kyle, Nick and Ken are 2–2; Grant and Luc are 1–3; Sarah is 0–4.

There is plenty for the league to consider before Thursday. Grant, under this newspaper’s ruling, will have an opportunity to consider it outdoors.

The dogs have declined to hear his explanation of the matchup. They have heard “walk” and consider the matter settled.
