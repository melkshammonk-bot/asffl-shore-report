Kitchen Nightmares: Drake & Bake leaves dinner in the fridge

Week 7 Reaction · October 27, 2026
Filed by The Shore Report’s regular beat reporter

A fictional celebrity guest appearance. All guest dialogue is invented for parody.

The first inspection lasted until Gordon Ramsay found Carlie’s bench.

“Seventy-seven points. Sitting here. And what went out?”

The Shore Report supplied the final score. The Trading Block beat Drake & Bake 158.65–135.75, taking Paul to 6–1 and Carlie to 2–5. Her nonstarters had contributed 77.45, the largest unserved collection in the league.

Ramsay looked from the bench to Nico Collins’s 0.60.

“It’s raw!”

Our reporter explained that Collins had actually finished playing.

“Then why does it look like we’ve only just taken it out?”

The guest requested five kitchens, the results sheet and somebody who could explain the flex positions. The newspaper supplied the first two. Nobody volunteered for the third.

Kitchen one: Drake & Bake has ingredients. Paul has dinner.

Carlie had begun service with 30.65 from Drake Maye, good for QB of the Week. Zay Flowers added 27.70 and Tucker Kraft 14.80. There was perfectly respectable food leaving parts of this kitchen.

Then Collins supplied six-tenths, Keenan Allen 6.10 and Matthew Golden 6.90. On the bench, KC Concepcion Jr. scored 23.20, collecting WR Benchwarmer of the Week.

“You’ve called it Drake & Bake. The Drake’s done his job. Who’s supervising the bloody bake?”

Carlie received Worst Manager, at 81% of the report’s 167.15-point hindsight maximum. That combination would have beaten Paul by 8.50. It required several different selections, including a Thursday decision; it was not a rescue available once every result was known.

The reporter pointed out that selecting Collins before kickoff did not come with a warning that he would score 0.60.

Ramsay accepted this, then asked why fantasy football did not issue warnings more generally. We referred him to Gordie.

Across the pass, Paul collected Highest Scorer for the second straight week. Jaxon Smith-Njigba’s 30.30, Trey McBride’s 26.10 and Minnesota’s 14.00 brought him WR, TE and DEF of the Week. Omar Cooper, promoted from last week’s bench, supplied another 19.50.

“Finally, some good food.”

Bryce Young scored 6.05 and Kenneth Walker 8.70. Ramsay examined the total again.

“You’ve served the best dinner in the building with that quarterback? Fine. Don’t show me the preparation area.”

Paul’s request for the guest’s qualifications was considered unnecessary on this occasion. The plate had arrived with three references.

Kitchen two: Oliver wins service. Luc keeps the receipt.

Sleezy Sandbars beat I Love Pitts 156.80–150.10, taking Oliver to 5–2 and Luc to 2–5. The 6.70-point margin earned Oliver Narrow Victory.

Josh Jacobs supplied 20.10, Jordan Love 19.70, Colston Loveland 18.90 and Ashton Jeanty 18.60. Brandon Aubrey’s 14.00, and K of the Week, proved particularly useful in a matchup decided by less than seven.

Ramsay offered a small nod. Then the fridge opened.

Joe Burrow had 31.80 on Oliver’s bench, winning QB Benchwarmer of the Week. Makai Lemon added 18.70 there while Xavier Worthy scored 2.10 in the lineup.

“Last week, a perfect lineup. This week, the chef’s special is behind the milk. What happened between services?”

Oliver still won. Haleigh’s established front-office reunion has acquired a result worth celebrating and a fridge worth photographing.

Luc deserved a better review than his record suggests. Jameson Williams scored 26.10, Dak Prescott 25.40, Amon-Ra St. Brown 23.20 and Rhamondre Stevenson 21.50. Luc earned Overachiever, finishing 26% above the report’s 118.70 expectation.

He also replaced the unavailable DeVonta Smith with Kendrick Bourne before the final lineup. Bourne returned 1.90; San Francisco’s defense supplied zero.

“One-point-nine and an empty bowl,” Ramsay said quietly. “You were nearly there, for heaven’s sake.”

The guest declined to blame Luc’s chair. It had contributed exactly as much as the defense, but had not occupied a lineup slot.

Kitchen three: Nick passes. Kyle’s pantry is inspected.

Bam Bam Cam beat Quack Quack 129.75–105.20. Nick improves to 4–3; Kyle falls to 3–4.

Quinshon Judkins supplied 23.40, Javonte Williams 21.50, Puka Nacua 20.80 and Jaylen Warren 15.70. That was enough to carry Patrick Mahomes’s 6.65 and Travis Kelce’s 6.40 through service.

“Mahomes and Kelce. Thirteen-point-oh-five between them. Come on!”

Nick nevertheless received Best Manager, one week after Worst Manager. The report rounded his efficiency to 100%, although 129.75 was 0.20 short of its 129.95 maximum. This was almost every available point, not literal perfection.

Ramsay studied last week’s certificate, then this week’s.

“Same kitchen?”

The reporter confirmed it.

“Right. So it can be done.”

Kyle’s Below Expectation award recorded a 34% shortfall against 159.60. London led his starters with 18.60; Braelon Allen contributed 17.40 and Tyler Shough 15.60. Garrett Wilson’s 4.60 and Coker’s 1.90 left the plate looking sparse. Jaylin Noel’s −0.30 removed a little of what was already on it.

“The garnish’s eaten the dinner.”

Ramsay demanded a look at the bench. Every nonstarter had scored zero. Daniels and Lawrence were on bye; Hall had been declared inactive and moved out of the lineup.

The reporter explained that an unavailable player was not a secret ingredient Kyle had neglected to use. There was no hidden winning meal in this pantry.

“Absolutely dreadful,” Ramsay said, examining the output. “But I’m not telling him to cook a man who isn’t available.”

Kyle’s advice service will presumably reopen once somebody asks how efficiently he arranged the disappointment.

Kitchen four: Lakota has the lamb. Ken has one enormous portion.

Giant Expectations beat Big Apple 154.95–117.10, taking Lakota to 6–1 with her fifth straight win. Ken is 3–4.

“Where’s the lamb sauce?”

At Lakota’s end of the counter, as it happened. CeeDee Lamb supplied 24.40, alongside Tee Higgins’s 26.80, Bucky Irving’s 22.00 and Tre Tucker’s 20.30. Cook and Coleman were on bye. The kitchen remained open.

“Four proper courses. Nobody explaining that the bread basket is the main event. Good.”

Ken had an exceptional main ingredient: Tony Pollard’s 35.50, the week’s best started running-back score and RB of the Week. McCaffrey supplied 22.00. Henry managed 4.90, Likely 2.20, and Raymond finished Thursday with zero.

“Thirty-five and a half from Pollard, and the whole kitchen makes a hundred and seventeen? Who’s watering this down?”

Ken’s twenty-seven-dollar Raymond purchase attracted an inspection of the receipt. Ramsay could not find a point on either side of it.

Ramsay asked whether the twenty-seven dollars included delivery. Ken would still like the points delivered.

Lakota’s Carolina defense also scored zero, with Detroit’s 5.00 waiting on her bench. She had enough elsewhere to absorb it. Ken did not.

Kitchen five: Grant brings three courses before the weekend

Boot Tuten Boogie beat Dartastic 145.20–97.35, earning Biggest Blowout by 47.85. Grant reaches 4–3, his third consecutive win. Sarah is 0–7 and received Lowest Scorer.

Grant’s Caleb Williams, Luther Burden and Swift had already supplied 64.90 on Thursday. Taylor added 19.00 and Pickens 15.70. This kitchen had most of its prep done while the guest was still looking for parking.

“Twenty-five-point-two from the quarterback. Twenty-three-point-seven from Burden. Sixteen from Swift. Thank you. People actually working.”

Sunday morning’s free-agent switch from Juwan Johnson to T.J. Hockenson supplied another 12.50. Grant’s twenty-one-dollar Tyler Warren was ruled out and stayed on the bench. That was unavailable stock, not an ingredient he could reasonably be expected to serve.

Sarah brought Rice out of the starting lineup, added Darius Cooper and received 2.70 from the replacement. Goff supplied 19.05, Barkley returned 14.10, Henry 12.10 and new kicker McLaughlin 12.00. Skattebo’s 18.10 remained on the bench, earning RB Benchwarmer of the Week.

There were points to rearrange in hindsight, but no combination caught Grant. Brown and Henry had also already played in Thursday’s flex slots; the kitchen could not simply move every finished plate when Sunday’s scores arrived.

On learning that Sarah had fed the Thanksgiving gathering, Ramsay lowered his voice.

“She’s already catered for this lot? And you’ve sent her another forty-seven-point defeat? Change the bookings.”

The Giants defense supplied two points. The guest inspected that particular bowl without directing his remarks at the hostess.

Transactions: no mystery meat

No completed trades appeared in the Week 7 or Week 8 feeds checked for this edition. No players, picks or FAAB changed hands in a trade there. The Trading Block continues to provide dinner while declining to function as a loading dock.

The actual shopping had consequences. Oliver’s zero-dollar Flournoy claim supplied 11.00 on his bench. Grant’s free-agent Hockenson went straight onto the plate for 12.50. Sarah’s McLaughlin supplied twelve, replacing the bye-week Bass.

Kyle’s five-dollar Shough scored 15.60. The Noel addition returned minus-three-tenths. Carlie’s seven-dollar Keenan Allen returned 6.10, while nineteen-dollar Antonio Williams was on bye. A bye is a scheduled closed kitchen, not evidence that an owner forgot to turn on the oven.

Carlie dropped Dameon Pierce on Tuesday, after his 10.80 bench points were already part of the completed Week 7 record. The newspaper has kept that sequence intact.

Ramsay asked whether any of the receipts came with a tasting portion. We declined to make a waiver claim on his behalf.
