Sarah wins Thanksgiving

Grant, Carlie, Nick, Lakota, Ken and Sarah gathered at the cottage for Thanksgiving football. It was cold. Sarah supplied a turkey dinner good enough to settle one of the weekend’s awards without consulting the app.

The Shore Report awards her Best Turkey Dinner. The fantasy schedule has declined to show comparable gratitude.

Dartastic scored 155.25, Sarah’s best total of the season. Jared Goff supplied 30.65, Mike Evans 25.30 and Tyler Warren 20.40. She could have beaten five other teams. Unfortunately, the one assigned to her was Kyle.

Quack Quack chose this occasion to produce 211.65, the league’s first 200-point score of the season, winning by 56.40. Sarah improved again. The opposition escalated.

Kyle collected Highest Scorer, Biggest Blowout and Overachiever, finishing 34% above his 157.92 projection. Last week he was Below Expectation. This week he has supplied enough evidence to resume giving advice at an entirely unreasonable volume.

Jahmyr Gibbs led the effort with 36.70 and RB of the Week. Parker Washington added 30.40, while Garrett Wilson delivered 23.40 immediately after arriving from Luc. Even the $42 Braelon Allen investment supplied 16.60. Several invoices have become easier to discuss.

There was no winning combination Sarah had overlooked. Her best possible lineup would have reached 162.05, still well short. Kyle moves to 3–2; Sarah remains 0–5, despite producing the fifth-highest score of the week.

Sarah remains unable to buy a fantasy win. Her application to have catering included in the standings is considerably stronger.

Paul’s first defeat gets an audience

The cottage was behind Lakota. Paul’s unbeaten run had reached the stage where its continuation was becoming a community concern.

Giant Expectations beat The Trading Block 156.45–104.90, a 51.55-point verdict in The Pylon’s Matchup of the Week. Lakota has won three straight. Paul has lost for the first time. Both are 4–1, although the cottage appears to have enjoyed arriving at those identical records rather differently.

Paul reportedly took the fantasy analysts’ advice and started three New Orleans Saints. Chris Olave supplied 8.60, Devaughn Vele 4.60 and Alvin Kamara 0.60: 13.80 combined.

Paul sought advice from the experts and ended up requiring divine intervention.

Jaxon Smith-Njigba’s 21.80 and Trey McBride’s 19.60 could only do so much. Paul finished 28% below his 145.30 projection, collecting Below Expectation. The experts have not been awarded a share of it.

Lakota had a much broader support network. Bucky Irving’s 31.50 was already banked on Thursday; Matthew Stafford added 31.15 on Monday. James Cook, Sam LaPorta and Kyren Williams contributed another 57.20 between them. CeeDee Lamb and Tee Higgins could combine for only 6.10 without bringing down the operation.

Gordie called them “Giant Expectations, not Giant Exaggerations.” Three consecutive wins and the end of Paul’s unbeaten run have made that an increasingly economical assessment.

The unnecessary emergency

At 7:32 p.m. Monday, roughly 43 minutes before the scheduled Bills–Rams kickoff, Nick traded active Keon Coleman to his fiancée Lakota for DJ Moore, who had already been declared inactive.

Lakota was still trailing 104.90–76.20. She put Coleman in a receiver slot and received 7.70. This was a move made before the result was settled, with points still required.

The difficulty for the paper’s inquiry arrived with the final arithmetic. Remove Coleman’s points altogether and Lakota still wins by 43.85. Moore sat on Nick’s bench; Nick’s starting lineup was unaffected.

Neither winner changed. The investigation has established considerable urgency and remarkably little consequence.

Maye supplies the suspense. Monday supplies the demolition.

Carlie had good reason to keep watching.

Drake Maye scored 43.20 on Sunday, winning QB of the Week and leading every starter in the league. After last week’s QB Benchwarmer award, he had made a persuasive case for being allowed into Drake & Bake.

Carlie finished her scoring at 141.30. Nick entered Monday on 122.30, nineteen points behind, with Josh Allen, Puka Nacua and Dalton Kincaid still to play.

They supplied 68.00.

Allen’s 31.60 and Puka’s 32.70 did most of the demolition; Kincaid added 3.70. Bam Bam Cam finished with a 190.30–141.30 win, the margin expanding to 49.00. Nick moves to 3–2, Carlie to 2–3. Sunday’s suspense was real. So was Monday’s removal of it.

Puka took WR of the Week, while Jason Myers’s 23.00 earned K of the Week. Nick also had Jaylen Warren’s 33.80 already working for him. Carlie’s quarterback had done an enormous amount; Nick had more departments contributing.

Carlie and Nick remained friends. Luka, Grant and Carlie’s dog, repeatedly applied for a closer relationship.

Grant gets back to winning

After losing to Carlie by fifty, Grant found a considerably more agreeable family result.

Boot Tuten Boogie beat the Sleezy Sandbars 162.70–145.70, ending Oliver’s three-matchup winning streak and moving Grant to 2–3. Oliver is 3–2, still comfortably ahead of last season’s entire two-win return, but temporarily unable to extend the comparison.

Grant replaced Tyler Shough with free-agent Jacoby Brissett on Sunday and received 28.20. Christian Watson scored 31.30, George Pickens’s Thursday contribution held at 28.00, and Spencer Shrader added 21.00. Jonathan Taylor and D’Andre Swift supplied another 28.50 between them. The recovery had several useful parts.

It also survived Roman Wilson leaving his game with a finger injury and 0.00.

Grant earned Best Manager, reaching 97% of his best possible 167.60. That award compares the selected lineup with the best combination after every score is known; it gives the computer an advantage unavailable on Sunday.

Oliver had 27.10 from Malik Nabers, 26.50 from Joe Burrow and 21.10 from Davante Adams, but George Kittle, Colston Loveland and Jake Ferguson combined for 13.20. Three tight ends had produced less than one good afternoon’s work.

Even Oliver’s optimal lineup would have lost by 12.15. Grant also received Narrow Victory for the actual seventeen-point margin, the closest result of a week in which suspense generally finished early.

Luc loves Pitts at a safe distance

Ken’s shopping produced modest returns. His football team produced a win.

Big Apple beat I Love Pitts 136.00–100.00, moving Ken to 3–2 and Luc to 1–4. The $6 Jakobi Meyers purchase supplied 5.70. The earlier $27 Kalif Raymond investment contributed 1.40 from the bench.

The Harrisons defence remains: “At that price, you can’t leave it there.” Terry McLaurin’s 12.20, good for WR Benchwarmer of the Week, and Khalil Shakir’s 11.00 suggest that useful stock was already available at home.

Fortunately, Isaiah Likely delivered 29.40 and TE of the Week, Green Bay scored 22.00 for DEF of the Week, and Derrick Henry added 21.00.

Across the matchup, I Love Pitts had entrusted its tight-end position to T.J. Hockenson.

Hockenson scored 1.70. Kyle Pitts scored 15.20 on the bench, taking TE Benchwarmer of the Week. Gordie had written, “The affection remains. Selection is a separate matter.” Luc has demonstrated an almost professional commitment to keeping them separate.

Starting Pitts would have added 13.50, but Luc would still have lost by 22.50. The team name alone could not fix this.

Luc collected Lowest Scorer and Worst Manager, reaching 71% of his 141.40 hindsight maximum. Getting there required several changes, including help from Rhamondre Stevenson and Blake Corum. His bench led the league with 50.50 points.

This was an unusually well-equipped defeat.

Elsewhere in the awards cabinet

Kyle’s Jayden Daniels took QB Benchwarmer of the Week with 33.25, ahead of Jordan Love’s 26.95. Nick’s MarShawn Lloyd earned RB Benchwarmer of the Week with 16.70, just 0.20 ahead of Luc’s Stevenson. Lakota’s Raiders defense retained DEF Benchwarmer of the Week with 4.00.

The clipboard’s Thanksgiving return

Gordie finished 4–1 on winners and 3–2 against his lines. Lakota, Ken and Kyle covered. Carlie was his only wrong winner; Grant won by 17 against a 17½-point line.

The clipboard department considers the missing half point a failure of implementation.

Paul remains the season scoring leader with 805.65, but Kyle has reduced the gap to 29.10. Paul and Lakota lead at 4–1; Kyle, Nick, Ken and Oliver are 3–2; Grant and Carlie are 2–3; Luc is 1–4; Sarah is 0–5.

Those are the official standings. The cottage has grounds to submit a correction.

Sarah fed everybody and won Thanksgiving. If the standings would like to argue, they can bring their own dinner.
