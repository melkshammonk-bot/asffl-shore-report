Please put the remote down

By the late window on Sunday, Ken was no longer changing channels. He was weighing the consequences.

Every time he switched games, something seemed to go wrong with his fantasy team. What began as an ordinary afternoon of watching football had become a situation in which pressing “last channel” required more consideration than drafting the team in the first place.

Big Apple eventually lost 131.80–110.65 to Kyle’s Quack Quack. There is no evidence the remote caused the 21.15-point defeat, but Ken would be entitled to remove the batteries until Thursday.

Kyle, meanwhile, received Sleeper’s Best Manager award for getting 96% of his best possible lineup score. One week after the Waddle incident, the technical department has obtained a certificate. He hardly needed the encouragement.

Jaylen Waddle contributed 19.80, a welcome return to the business of producing points. Quack Quack leaves Kyle’s last weekend in Canada with a win and the prospect of thawing out down south. Apparently the migration goes more smoothly when the duck participates.

Nick wins everything that doesn’t count

Nick collected three Benchwarmer awards: Patrick Mahomes at quarterback, Travis Kelce at tight end, and Jacksonville’s defense. His bench produced 76.70 points, the most in the league.

A petition to start 17 players instead of 11 would be entirely understandable. At the very least, it would allow Nick to stop congratulating employees who were not on shift.

For fairness, Josh Allen scored 42.30 in the starting lineup and won QB of the Week. Leaving Mahomes’s 32.80 on the bench was the correct decision. Nick had excellent quarterback management and a 190.70–123.10 loss to Paul. Both things are allowed to happen, although it feels impolite.

The Pylon’s Matchup of the Week became a 67.60-point win for The Trading Block. Gordie picked Paul by two and a half. He identified the correct vehicle; its speed appears to have surprised him.

Paul led the league in scoring for the second straight week and led Week 2 transaction activity with four completed moves. Jaxon Smith-Njigba supplied 41.50, while free-agent pickup Dalton Schultz scored 24.00 and won TE of the Week.

Schultz had briefly belonged to Luc, who dropped him while claiming Kyle Pitts for $21 in FAAB. Pitts scored 2.50 in Luc’s lineup. Paul then picked up Schultz. There were no completed trades this week, so Paul has discovered a way to benefit from somebody else’s roster decision without even sending an offer.

The defending champion is now the league’s only 2–0 manager. On the available evidence, next year’s draft may need to be held in Albania. The oceanfront was lovely, but apparently the competitive advantage was elsewhere.

The answer is on the bench again

Lakota has now tried both quarterbacks. The quarterbacks have tried her patience.

In Week 1, she started Matthew Stafford while Justin Herbert did better on the bench. In Week 2, she started Herbert and got 6.80. Stafford, restored to the position of helpful spectator, scored 30.25.

Giant Expectations lost 137.40–121.70 to Grant’s Boot Tuten Boogie. This time the decision changed the outcome: with Stafford in Herbert’s place and everything else unchanged, Lakota would have won 145.15–137.40.

She has identified two quarterbacks capable of helping her. The remaining difficulty is getting either to do it during office hours.

Grant won the week’s closest matchup, helped by 28.20 from Jonathan Taylor and 25.00 from New England’s defense, both positional award winners. He also finished his exams this past week. Now he can REALLY focus on Carlie’s team.

Drake & Bake lost 152.85–81.00 to Oliver’s Sleezy Sandbars, finishing with the league’s lowest score and its largest defeat. Carlie must have taken the team to an Airbnb, because they certainly didn’t show up for the matchup. Evan McPherson, her kicker, was her highest-scoring starter with 12.00. At least somebody found the address.

Oliver, led by 38.50 from Davante Adams, already has half his entire win total from last season. It is September.

Haleigh prefers watching Housewives to watching Oliver’s fantasy team. A 71.85-point win is an encouraging development, but Assistant GM is a management position, not an obligation to sit through every episode.

Goff and running

Luc beat Sarah’s Dartastic 163.85–93.20, with Amon-Ra St. Brown contributing 34.20 and Dak Prescott 32.55. His Cowboys rain dance finally ended Monday night. The secret to a successful rain dance, of course, is to keep dancing until it rains. Scientific standards remain modest in the Cowboys community.

His loyalty does have limits: Luc claimed the Rams’ defense and dropped Dallas. Supporting the Cowboys is one thing. Giving them a place in the lineup requires a separate discussion.

With Dart headed for the sidelines and Jared Goff’s 31.05 points sitting on her bench, Sarah is Goff and running with her new team name: Winstontastic.

Paul is unbeaten, eight managers are crowded together at 1–1, and Sarah has naming rights to whatever comes next.

Ken should be allowed to read this edition without anyone touching the television.
