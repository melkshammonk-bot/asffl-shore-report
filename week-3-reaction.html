The student has requested the good end of the couch

Nick helped Lakota prepare for the draft. On Sunday, she returned his investment.

Giant Expectations beat Bam Bam Cam 162.40–107.70, a 54.70-point household inspection that produced the largest margin in the league. The Couch Bowl has a winner. The seating arrangements can be negotiated separately.

After two weeks of starting whichever quarterback would rather be on her bench, Lakota put Matthew Stafford back in and received 24.80 points and QB of the Week. Justin Herbert scored 15.00 from the bench. Both men finally appear to have read the schedule.

Stafford was also her highest-scoring starter, but this was a collective eviction notice: Tee Higgins supplied 21.00, Kyren Williams 20.80 and CeeDee Lamb 20.20. Harrison Mevis added 16.00 and the kicker award. Lakota could have started Herbert and still won comfortably. This time, getting the quarterback right was a luxury.

Across the couch, Nick had 17.50 from Josh Allen and 2.80 from Dalton Kincaid, with Patrick Mahomes’s 18.40 and Travis Kelce’s 13.90 sitting out. There is a reasonable question about the Kansas City alternative. There is also an answer: switching both, with everything else unchanged, would have brought Nick to 119.70. He would still have lost by 42.70.

The bench could have improved the conversation. It could not have changed the subject.

Gordie had picked Nick by four and a half in The Pylon’s Matchup of the Week. He correctly identified the household in which something significant would happen.

Dad will take that off your hands

Ken’s contribution to family harmony was a 165.70–111.15 win over Grant. Big Apple moves to 2–1; Boot Tuten Boogie drops to 1–2. The father who claims not to do research has once again made preparation look faintly unnecessary.

There was even a small collection service. Grant dropped Michael Pittman to add Terrance Ferguson; Ken claimed Pittman for $0. Pittman stayed on Ken’s bench with 3.60 points, so this was not the reason Grant lost. His father simply beat him and took something home.

The more expensive acquisition worked. Grant’s $16 waiver pickup Tyler Shough scored 24.35, leading his starters. The tight-end choice did not: Ferguson produced 1.90, while Juwan Johnson’s 18.30 remained on the bench. Sleeper put Grant at 80% of his best possible lineup, the week’s lowest efficiency. Even its perfect-lineup total of 139.75 would have lost to Ken.

There are afternoons when correcting your mistakes merely produces a tidier defeat.

Ken, meanwhile, left 66.95 points on the bench, the most in the league, including Benchwarmer winners Brock Purdy at 34.25 and Harold Fannin at 22.10. The victory has considerably reduced interest in why they were sitting there. Michael Wilson’s 24.90 led the people actually working.

Gordie’s instruction to leave Baltimore–Dallas on the television had a respectable return: Lamar Jackson and Derrick Henry combined for 43.20. We cannot establish whether Ken obeyed. The remote remains a person of interest, but it is entitled to a better lawyer after this result.

Kyle wins the week. Paul keeps the building.

Quack Quack beat Carlie’s Drake & Bake 176.70–136.65, giving Kyle the league’s highest score and moving him to 2–1. Jahmyr Gibbs scored 41.40 for RB of the Week; Brock Bowers added 26.60 for the tight-end award, and Drake London supplied 27.40.

Kyle now has statistical support for the advice he was going to give everyone anyway.

Carlie improved by 55.65 points on last week and still lost by 40.05. That is a lot of progress to have to explain as another loss. Bijan Robinson’s 34.30, Matthew Golden’s 21.00 and Denver’s 18.00 were substantial contributions. Drake Maye’s 3.75 was less helpful to the naming department. Drake & Bake is now 1–2, despite the baking having improved considerably.

Paul did not win the weekly scoring prize. He merely beat Luc 138.55–104.80, stayed the only unbeaten manager at 3–0, and collected Best Manager for reaching 97% of his best possible lineup.

His 527.10 points through three weeks lead the league by 81.35 over Kyle. The early-favourite discussion is beginning to resemble a reading of the standings. There is no need to award the championship in September; Paul already has enough things.

Jaxon Smith-Njigba’s 33.50 earned WR of the Week. Luc finished with the league’s lowest score, despite 23.70 from Garrett Wilson and 20.70 from Dak Prescott. De’Von Achane managed 1.70, while Kyle Pitts produced 1.50. The $21 Pitts investment has now followed last week’s 2.50 with an even smaller instalment.

Schultz, the tight end Luc released before Paul picked him up, scored 6.00 for Paul this time. No spectacular revenge performance was required. Luc had provided enough assistance elsewhere.

The Trading Block remains the name on the door. First place remains occupied.

Oliver completes last season ahead of schedule

Oliver beat his stepmother’s Dartastic 137.80–118.15, moving the Sleezy Sandbars to 2–1 and matching his entire two-win 2025 season before October.

There is still quite a lot of schedule left. This is a new administrative problem.

George Kittle led him with 26.20, followed by Ja’Marr Chase at 23.80 and Davante Adams at 20.70. Sarah did put Jared Goff in, and his 22.05 led her starters. The lineup department did its job at quarterback; the first win remains outstanding at 0–3.

Their 19.65-point margin qualified as suspense by this week’s standards: it was the closest. Nobody required a recount.

Paul stands alone at 3–0. Kyle, Lakota, Ken and Oliver form the 2–1 chasing group; Grant, Luc, Nick and Carlie are 1–2.

Lakota, meanwhile, has solved the quarterback question for one week and beaten the person who helped her study.

Nick should ask permission before offering advice from the other end of the couch.
