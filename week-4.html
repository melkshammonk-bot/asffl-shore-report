THE PYLON’S LINES: WEEK 4

October 1, 2026 · Gordie “The Pylon” MacNeil
Senior Road Crew Stop/Slow Technician

Right. Three winners out of five again. I did put Nick’s name on a matchup he lost by nearly fifty-five, but the other three winners shouldn’t have to suffer because I inspected the wrong end of the couch.

That's enough about that.

This week Grant plays Carlie. They’re both one and two and they live together. I’ve worked on shared driveways. The measurements are never the real problem.

The Pylon’s Matchup of the Week

Boot Tuten Boogie vs. Drake & Bake

My line: Drake & Bake -2½
My winner: Drake & Bake

Grant helps Carlie with fantasy advice. This week I’m requiring him to identify which team the advice is intended to improve.

Nothing accusatory. We mark buried cables for the same reason.

The difficulty is that good advice could cost him the matchup, while bad advice could cost him considerably more of the evening. I recommend answering only the question asked. This is sound practice around machinery and people you live with.

Carlie appears quite capable of making her own arrangements. She’s put Sam Darnold in for Drake Maye. Cost her a dollar. Maye gave her three and three-quarter points last week, and Drake & Bake is now proceeding without the Drake. I’ve seen a company keep the name after the original owner stopped being useful.

She also outscored Grant last week. Both lost, so it didn’t settle much officially, but I expect a person could get considerable use out of that information without raising her voice.

I’m giving her the right of way by two and a half. I trust Bijan to do a substantial part of the work, which leaves the awkward business until Monday: Grant has Shough throwing to Juwan Johnson in the same game. We could get through the entire weekend and still have two people in one house requiring opposite things from a clock.

That’s where Grant’s advice will need to become very specific. “We need a touchdown here” is no longer an adequate instruction.

Giant Expectations vs. Smack Dak ASB

My line: Giant Expectations -3½
My winner: Giant Expectations

Luc has Dak. Lakota has CeeDee Lamb. A Dallas touchdown to Lamb can improve Luc’s Sunday and damage it in the same movement.

This is where the Chair Protocol becomes difficult.

As I understand it, Luc has one approved seat when Dallas has the ball and another when they don’t. Once a drive starts, he cannot change seats. Whether a commercial break permits relocation remains unresolved. I asked for the rules from before the last Cowboys loss. Apparently those are no longer in effect.

I can administer that. What I cannot settle is where he’s meant to sit when his quarterback throws a touchdown to Lakota.

For now I’m ruling that he stays put and experiences both feelings in the same chair.

I’m taking Lakota by three and a half. Cook and Kyren give me more confidence in her ground crew, though this assumes her questionable Moore and Bucky are available. Luc can get excellent work from Dak and still spend the afternoon watching some of it arrive at Lakota’s address. I don’t like that arrangement for a man already operating under furniture restrictions.

No chair adjustment is included in the line. I need a floor plan before I accept responsibility for a sectional.

Bam Bam Cam vs. Big Apple

My line: Bam Bam Cam -2½
My winner: Bam Bam Cam

Ken beat his son Grant last week. Now he gets his stepson Nick. I’m checking the fixture list for a family rate.

The man who claims not to research has paid twenty-seven dollars for Kalif Raymond and put him straight to work. That’s a fairly specific accident.

Ken says he doesn't research. That's fine. Twenty-seven dollars still went out the door and Kalif Raymond came back. That's a fair bit of coincidence for a Thursday.

Nick could do the rest of us a service by beating him before this becomes a method Kyle has to explain. I think he will, narrowly: Allen and his receivers are enough for me, provided Jefferson and Puka both play. Both are questionable. Without them, my two-and-a-half-point line needs another inspection.

If Ken wins, I’m leaving the research question alone. A man can only be helped so far with his explanation.

Quack Quack vs. Sleezy Sandbars

My line: Quack Quack -11½
My winner: Quack Quack

Oliver has matched last season’s two wins already. His uncle Kyle is next, also at two and one. This ought to be an opportunity for encouragement, but Kyle appears to require the encouragement himself.

He won last week’s scoring title and promptly paid forty-two dollars for Braelon Allen. Breece Hall is doubtful, so I understand the repair. I’m still writing “temporary access road” on the invoice. It’s important he doesn’t start calling it an investment in the community.

Oliver has reached his entire previous win total; Kyle has reached the conclusion that last week’s highest-scoring team needed urgent work. They are approaching the same record from quite different directions.

I prefer Kyle by eleven and a half. Gibbs and Bowers did enough last week to earn my confidence. The forty-two dollars has mainly earned my interest.

Oliver can take a third win without my permission. Kyle should understand that a third win does not come with permission to explain everybody else’s two.

We'll all manage.

Dartastic vs. The Trading Block

My line: The Trading Block -14½
My winner: The Trading Block

Sarah is looking for her first win. Paul hasn’t lost. I’d normally put a temporary light here and give her a turn, but apparently the league insists on playing it out.

Paul has Bryce Young starting ahead of Hurts and thirty-five-dollar pickup Ollie Gordon on the bench. Being three and oh gives a man room to make decisions that would require a written explanation from the rest of us.

Mind you, winning is a fairly good answer to most questions.

The trouble is that Smith-Njigba and McBride give me every reason to let him through again. Paul by fourteen and a half, which is an unpleasant thing to write when Sarah has already waited three weeks.

I would be pleased to be wrong for her. It would also give Paul an opportunity to explain the thirty-five dollars under less favourable lighting.

That’s the sheet. Grant and Carlie, I can direct traffic through a disputed driveway. I cannot make the person waiting believe the other person has had exactly the same amount of time.

Keep the entrance clear. Whoever wins will need room to come home as though nothing has happened.
